# ADR-225 — Blog subscriptions notify on publish, and there is no broadcast composer (29.08.2026)

**Context.** The blog/newsletter research (Ghost, Substack, beehiiv, Buttondown) shows the segment's center of gravity is email: composer, campaigns, segmentation, deliverability teams. Cedar Clerk already had the pieces at showcase scope — `ShowcaseFollower` double opt-in, the Resend path — and readers of the *blog* had no way to hear about a new post short of RSS. The tempting shape is "a newsletter product"; the honest question is what a $3–6/month margin and a shared sending domain can carry.

**Decision.** Blog-wide subscription is **notify-on-publish only**:

1. `BlogSubscriber` generalizes the showcase follower machinery: double opt-in via `/subscribe*`, an unsubscribe link in every mail, per-tenant list.
2. The only thing that ever sends is the **"Notify subscribers" toggle at a post's first blog publish** — never on re-publish or edit, and never for a private post, whatever the toggle says. Sends go through a durable `BlogNotifyJob` queue drained by a Quartz minute job, so a crash mid-run neither loses nor doubles a batch.
3. **There is no broadcast composer** — no campaigns, no segments, no "email my list" free-text send. The mail is a pointer to a published post, not a second rendering surface.

**Reasoning.** Margin: email is a per-unit cost against a fixed-price plan; a broadcast feature makes that cost unbounded and user-controlled — the same trap ADR-092 built credits to avoid, without a metering story here. Deliverability: arbitrary broadcast content is what gets a shared sending domain classified as bulk mail; a one-mail-per-published-post pattern is transactional-shaped and stays clean, and one tenant's habits must not poison every other tenant's opt-in confirmations. Product: the blog is the content — a mail that *is* the content would be a parallel surface the renderers don't cover.

**Consequence.** A subscriber receives at most one mail per new public post, which is a promise the subscribe box can print. The deliberate cost is that "send my subscribers something" is unrepresentable; if a real newsletter is ever wanted, it arrives as a new ADR with a metering answer, not as a checkbox.
