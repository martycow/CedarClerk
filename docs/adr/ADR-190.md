# ADR-190 — The Export window and the Integrations panel are shelves, and a stamp says which account is live (22.08.2026)

**Context.** Two surfaces were left behind by the Cedar Bench port. Both are still drawn in the
pre-bench vocabulary — a `1px solid var(--border)` hairline, `--radius-md`, a flat `--sheet` — and
both stand out against every screen ported since ADR-136, which say what they are made of.

The Export window states its sequence with a CSS circle (`.export-step-no`) and a 15px heading. The
Integrations panel is six `.integration-block` boxes drawn identically while doing three different
jobs: `@CedarClerkBot` is a *reading* (reachable or not — nothing about it is connectable), Channels
is a *list* that needs four times the height of anything else, and the remaining four are
*accounts*. ADR-095 already fixed the placement complaint — one shape for every network, disconnect
in the head, connect in the body — and that shape is exactly what now makes the panel unreadable:
six identical boxes, and the only thing separating a live account from an empty one is a grey line
of prose in the middle of each.

PRD's non-requirements refuse "general redesign as a monolithic item — concrete pains only".
These are two concrete pains, and Marty named them: *the integrations all look the same*, and *both
look flat, not bench*.

**Decision.**

1. **A step of the Export window is a shelf, and so is a network.** Both surfaces drop their own
   box classes and use `app-shelf-panel` — the component every dock, sidebar and inspector on the
   bench already is. `.export-step`, `.export-step-head`, `.export-step-no`, `.export-step-title`
   and `.integration-block*` are deleted rather than restyled: a second component that draws a
   shelf is the drift the kit exists to stop.

2. **The step number is carved into the sign, not pinned beside it.** `[title]="'1 · ' + …"`, so the
   tile reads `1 · WHICH VERSION` in the same display caps every other panel header uses.
   `ShelfPanel.prompt.md` gives the header exactly three slots — title, count, actions — and this
   adds no fourth. The brass numeral the design canvas drew is refused for that reason: it is a
   leading slot the component does not have, and one screen is not a reason to grow the kit.

3. **State is stamped on paper, never on the wood.** Each network's sheet opens with an
   `app-stamp-badge` — *Connected* in pine, *Not set up* dried — beside the brand mark and the
   account. Every stamp tone is an ink measured against `--surface`; there is no wood-safe tone and
   inventing one would put an unmeasured pair on the one surface `tools/check-contrast.mjs` scores
   hardest. The gain the sign tile was wanted for survives anyway: the shelves are identical in
   shape, so the stamps line up as a column the eye runs down.

4. **The bot stops pretending to be an integration.** It becomes a shelf of `app-spec-row` readings
   — status and role, label in sans and value in mono — which is what that component is: one ruled
   line of a printed spec sheet. Nothing on it connects, because nothing about it can be connected.

5. **The four accounts sit two to a row**, Telegram and Channels stay full width. Channels is a
   list and Telegram is the prerequisite for it; X, Bluesky and Discord are three short panels that
   left two thirds of a 700px column empty each.

6. **Prose leaves the header.** The three steps whose head carried a sentence (When, Private access,
   Files) move it to the first line of the sheet. Wood carries labels and counters, paper carries
   sentences — ADR-138's split, applied to text as well as to boxes.

**Rejected.** *Adding a leading-badge input to `ShelfPanelComponent`* — see 2; the kit is shared by
eleven screens and this one wanted a decoration. *Restyling `.integration-block` in bench colours* —
it would be a second shelf that looks like a shelf until the real one changes. *Moving Files and
Private access out of the Export window*, which the B and C directions of the design canvas both
did: they are genuinely not export (FI2.6's own rule), but relocating them is a migration through
the Posts Manager with its own inventory rows, and folding it into a re-skin would hide a
behavioural change inside a visual one. It stays on the backlog as its own item.

**Consequence.** Every panel in the application is now the same object, and the two screens that
predate the bench no longer announce themselves by being flat. Nothing about publishing, connecting
or disconnecting changed: the same handlers, the same endpoints, the same `sec-*` ids, so every
anchor into Settings and `UiInventoryDriftTests` still resolve. What did change is that "which of my
accounts is live" is now a column of stamps rather than six paragraphs.
