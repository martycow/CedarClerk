# ADR-083 — A long document becomes a thread, and the author decides where it breaks (T-106)

**Context** (01.08.2026): ADR-082 established that the design doc — 44,474 characters and 105 media against limits of 32,768 and ~10 — is not a Telegram post and cannot become one. Publishing refuses it honestly. T-106 is the other half: letting it go out as several messages.

**Decision: splitting is offered, never applied.** The switch is off by default, appears only when the post actually does not fit, and shows the parts before anything is sent — number, what each one opens with, its size and its media count. Turning one post into eight messages is a loud act in someone's channel, and the difference between a tool and a liability is whether the author saw it coming.

**Where the cuts land** (`TelegramThreadSplitter`, Core, 9 tests):
- **At a heading whenever one is near.** A message that ends mid-section reads like a transmission error; one that ends where a section ends reads like a chapter. So a part is closed at the next heading once it is 60% full, rather than filled to the brim and cut at an arbitrary character. This is the rule that turns "eight slices" into "eight sections", and it is why the preview is worth reading.
- **A block is never split.** A table, a code block, a list or a media group crosses no boundary.
- **Both limits count.** A hundred images will cut a thread long before its text does.
- A single block larger than the whole budget still ships as its own part: refusing there would leave a document that cannot be published and no explanation, and the network's own refusal is the better answer.

**What each message carries**: the part's own blocks, plus an `N/M` **footer**. A footer, not a heading — the first message should open with the post, not with its own bookkeeping, while a reader arriving mid-thread still learns there is more. The signature, the blog cross-link and the hashtags go on the **last part only**: repeated eight times they stop reading as a signature.

**How it hangs together**: each part replies to the previous one, so Telegram renders a thread rather than eight loose posts, and **only the first part notifies** — eight notifications for one publication is how a channel loses subscribers.

**One job per part** (extending ADR-081), and this is the decision that pays for itself: a thread that fails on part four must be resumable at part four, because retrying the whole publication would send parts one to three a second time and a channel cannot un-see them. A part runs only after its predecessor has succeeded; if an earlier part failed, the later ones are held back with "an earlier part did not go out" rather than leaving a channel with parts 1 and 3 of a document.

**A race this exposed, in code shipped hours earlier**: `Kick` is fire-and-forget, and inside a sweep it competed with the sweep's own loop — part 3 could be decided while part 2 was still running. The sweep now runs sequentially and does not kick successors; the kick exists only for immediacy, and the sweep is the deterministic path.

**Not done, deliberately**: a manual cut marker in the editor. It is the honest control for "break here" and it is a TipTap node, an authoring affordance and a rendering rule — a feature, not a detail of this one. The preview plus headings covers the case that exists today.
