# ADR-187 — The AI actions leave the tool strip for the sheet's own context menu (22.08.2026)

**Context.** The two AI actions — *Fix errors* and *Schizo-izer* — ship as a chip pinned to the tail
of the tool strip's first row, behind a popover, with the caption *AI · Pro Plus* under it. It is
the only chip on the strip that is not a formatting or insert command, it is pinned so the fit may
never move it, and it is the widest thing on the row because it doubles as a progress readout while
a job runs.

Marty: *I definitely DON'T LIKE how the AI button and popup look like. They don't belong there. They
should be somewhere else.* Asked where, he chose the selection context menu.

He is right about the belonging. The strip is a row of commands that act **where the caret is**, one
press each, instantly. Both AI actions are the opposite of all three: they act on the whole language
version, they cost a credit, and they take a confirm dialog and thirty seconds. A control that has
to explain itself before it will do anything is not a tool-strip button.

There is already a menu on the sheet, opened by right-click, and it holds exactly one entry —
*create a glossary term from the selection* (01.08.2026). One entry is not a menu, and a second
home for sheet-scoped commands would be the duplication `.claude/rules/ui-changes.md` rule 1 exists
to refuse. So the AI actions go into the one that exists.

**Decision.**

1. **The chip, its popover and its progress readout leave the strip**, and `aiActions` leaves the
   toolbar catalogue with them. Nothing replaces it on the row.

2. **The sheet's context menu opens on a right-click anywhere in the sheet**, not only over a
   selection. It was selection-only because its one entry needed a selection; with entries that do
   not, that condition would hide them exactly when nothing is picked. The glossary entry keeps its
   own condition and is drawn only when there is a selection to make a term out of.

3. **The AI entries say what they act on.** They act on the whole language version, not on the
   selected run, and a menu opened from a highlighted sentence would otherwise imply the sentence.
   The labels name the document; the confirm dialog already states the language and the word count,
   and it is unchanged.

4. **A running job is reported where it is now** — in the rule at the foot of the screen, through
   the save/status word — rather than by a progress bar living in a menu that is shut. The menu's
   entry becomes *Cancel* while a job runs, which is what the popover already did.

**Rejected.** *Making the AI act on the selection instead.* It is the better feature and it is not
this decision: the endpoint takes a document, the credit is priced per document, and the confirm
dialog's word count is the document's. Worth a backlog row, not a silent change of meaning. *A hook
on the wall.* The wall names screens, and AI is not one. *Leaving one entry in the strip and moving
the other.* Two homes for two halves of one feature.

**Consequence.** The strip goes back to being what it says it is — commands that act at the caret,
one press each — and the sheet's context menu becomes a real menu instead of a single-entry stub.
The cost is a click of discoverability: an action nobody right-clicks for is an action nobody finds,
which is the risk this entry accepts and names. ADR-150's fit is simplified by one pinned element it
no longer has to reserve room for.
