# ADR-166 — Outside the shell nothing declares the surface, so the door declares it itself

**Context.** Four screens live outside the bench shell by the shape of the route tree: `login`,
`register`, `terms` and `privacy` (ADR-139 clause 1). Everything the shell provides therefore does
not reach them — and two of those things are not decoration.

The first is the touch floor. `styles.scss` gives a control its 44px minimum by inheritance:
`[data-surface="paper"]` declares `--hit-surface`, and every control under it spends what it
inherits (ADR-156). The one element that declares it for the whole app is the shell's `main.body`.
Outside the shell there is no such ancestor, so on these four screens `--hit-surface` resolves to
nothing and every control stands at no floor at all. This is invisible in `check-density`, which
scores declared CSS and had nothing here to score, and invisible in a browser on a mouse.

The second is the theme toggle. ADR-151 moved it into the RailHeader's dots menu and said that menu
is the only place it lives. That is true inside the shell. These four pages each carry their own
toggle in the top-right corner, and they must: the login screen is themed before there is a session
(`ThemeService` applies the stored theme in its constructor), and a reader who wants the dark theme
to read the terms cannot reach a menu that is behind a login wall.

Beyond those two, the pages are simply unported: `24px`, `12px`, `30px`, `8px`, `34px 40px 28px`
written as literals, `.primary-btn` and `.field input` drawn by hand in three files, and four
`wk-decor` spans per auth page left over from the retired skin — scoped under an attribute nothing
sets since ADR-136, and already `display: none` in the page's own CSS.

**Decision.** The door is one sheet of paper on the bench wall, and it carries the two things the
shell would have carried.

**Attribution.** *Implementer's port under Stage 5.* Clause 2 is the one that is a decision rather
than a translation; the rest follows from the port's own rules.

1. **The sheet declares `data-surface="paper"`, and the wall does not.** The card is the paper; the
   ground it stands on is the bench wall, and the one control out there — the theme toggle — is
   chrome by its own variant. Putting the attribute on the card is what makes the floor arrive by
   inheritance rather than by a selector, which is the shape ADR-156 fixed. Writing the page's rules
   under `.card[data-surface="paper"]` rather than under a bare class is what puts these three
   screens inside `check-density`'s reach for the first time: a paper block is measured, and a page
   root that names no surface is not.

2. **The theme toggle stays, and it is the page's own.** ADR-151's rule is scoped to the shell,
   because the menu it names is part of the shell. Restating it here would leave a pre-auth reader
   with no way to switch themes at all. It is a `rail`-variant button, so it is chrome sitting on the
   wall rather than a control on the paper.

3. **The reading pages are set in the serif.** The material rule reserves the serif face for reading
   surfaces, and the terms and the privacy policy are the longest continuous prose in the product.
   They get `--font-serif` at `--fs-read`/`--lh-read`, which is the writing sheet's own setting. The
   two auth cards stay sans: a form is not a reading surface.

4. **The dead scenery is deleted, not restyled.** The eight `wk-decor` spans are markup for rules
   that can no longer match. Nothing replaces them: the wall's own lamp wash and the sheet's paper
   texture are what the bench uses to say the same thing.

5. **`shared/legal-page.component.*` is edited in place rather than replaced.** It has exactly two
   consumers and both are in this decision's scope; it is the terms and privacy screen, filed under
   `shared/` because two pages project into it.

**Rejected.** *Putting the four pages inside the shell so they inherit the surface.* The rail carries
a project switcher, save state and hooks into guarded screens; every one is meaningless without a
session, which is the whole reason ADR-139 put them outside. *Declaring `data-surface="paper"` on
`:root` so it always resolves.* Every chrome piece in the app is then a paper descendant that has to
out-declare it, which is the selector-pinning ADR-156 refused. *Dropping the per-page toggle and
letting the OS preference decide until sign-in.* The app stores an explicit choice and applies it
pre-auth already; the control would be the only place in the product where a stored preference could
be read but not changed.

**Consequence.** Two of the four screens now hold controls at 44px on a touch device where they
previously held them at whatever the content made them. On the auth cards that is invisible — the
fields were already taller — and on the legal pages it applies to two links and one button.

The debt this leaves is one input. `app-input` carries no `autocomplete`, no `maxlength` and no way
to be named by anything but a visible label of its own, so the login and register fields lost their
`autocomplete="username"`/`"current-password"` hints when they became bench fields, and the toolbar
searches that had only an `aria-label` are named by a visually-hidden `<label for>` the page draws
itself. That is a change to a shared primitive and is left to whoever owns it, named here rather
than worked around twice.
