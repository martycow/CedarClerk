# ADR-189 — Credits can be bought by the number, at the list rate; the packs stay a discount (22.08.2026)

**Context.** ADR-092's wallet sells credits in three fixed packs — 10 / 50 / 100 — and nothing else.
Marty asked for a field to buy an arbitrary number, noting he was not sure it was possible without
more Stripe setup. It is: the Stripe path already builds a `price_data` line item inline rather than
referencing a stored Price, so the amount is a number the server computes, not an object anyone has
to create in a dashboard. The Telegram Stars path builds its invoice inline too.

What actually blocks it is fulfilment, not checkout. Both paths credit the wallet by looking the
**pack** up again on the way back — `CreditPacks.Find(metadata[credits_pack])` in the webhook, and
the same call on the Stars payload — so a purchase with no pack behind it would take the money and
grant nothing. The amount has to travel with the payment, and it has to be a number the server put
there rather than one the browser sent.

The pricing question is the other half. The packs are not one rate: 10 credits cost 40¢ each, 50
cost 36¢, 100 cost 30¢. A custom amount priced at the best of those would make every pack pointless,
and priced at the worst it would look like a penalty.

**Decision.**

1. **The list rate is the smallest pack's rate** — 40¢ and 20 ⭐ per credit — and it lives in
   `CreditPacks` beside the packs, derived from the first entry rather than written twice. The packs
   are then exactly what they look like: a discount for buying more at once, 10% at fifty and 25% at
   a hundred.

2. **Bounds: 5 to 1000 credits.** Below five the payment provider's own fee is most of the charge;
   above a thousand it stops being a top-up, and a four-figure charge nobody meant to make is a
   refund conversation. Both are checked **on the server**, which is the only place that matters —
   the field's `min`/`max` are a courtesy to the person typing.

3. **The amount rides on the payment.** Stripe gets `metadata[credits_amount]`, the Stars payload
   gets `custom:<n>`, and the webhook and the bot each read the pack first and the amount second.
   The line item and the invoice are priced from the server's own arithmetic, never from a number in
   the request body — a client-supplied price is the oldest hole in this shape.

4. **One purchase path, not two.** The custom field sits with the packs and feeds the same
   payment-method step, the same buttons and the same ledger row. `CreditCheckoutRequest` gains an
   optional `Credits`; a request carrying neither a known pack nor a valid amount is a 400.

**Rejected.** *Stripe Payment Links or stored Prices per amount.* An object per amount is an object
per amount — the inline `price_data` the code already uses is what makes an arbitrary number
possible at all. *Letting the browser send the price.* The server prices it; the browser says how
many. *A slider.* A number is what someone buying credits knows and a slider is what they would have
to aim.

**Consequence.** The packs keep their job — they are the cheaper option, visibly — and the wallet
stops refusing money from someone who wants 27 credits. The two fulfilment paths now answer *how
many credits was this* from the payment itself, which is also what a future non-pack purchase (a
gift, a grant, a refund top-up) will need.
