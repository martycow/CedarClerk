# ADR-140 — Focus ring: two layers, because no single colour clears 3:1 on both paper and rail

**Context.** The mirror prescribes one flat ring: `--focus-outline: 2px solid var(--focus-ring)` at `--focus-offset: 2px` (`.design-sync/ds-v2/tokens/materials.css:27-28`) with `--focus-ring: var(--brass)` (`tokens/colors.css:71`). The app prescribes a different flat ring: one global `:focus-visible` of `2px solid var(--accent)` at 2px offset (`cedarclerk-web/src/styles.scss:269-272`), with `.tiptap` the sole exclusion (`styles.scss:277`) — ADR-074, restated at `docs/design/DESIGN.md:142`. Bench becoming the only look forces a choice between them, and it also changes the question: bench is the first look in which controls sit on *dark* chrome while the theme is light. A ring measured only against paper has never been the whole test.

Ratios below use the sRGB luminance formula of `cedarclerk-web/tools/check-contrast.mjs:67-78`, extended by hand for the two things that tool cannot do yet: gradients are evaluated at each end stop, and alpha is composited over the backdrop instead of being discarded (`resolveColor` drops the fourth `rgba()` component at `check-contrast.mjs:62`). The floor is 3:1, SC 1.4.11.

**Flat `--brass` #C69A4E fails on every reading surface in both themes** — `--paper` 2.39, `--paper-2` 2.15, `--paper-bright` 2.53, `--wall-hi` 2.21, `--wall-lo` 1.98, `--leaf-bg` 1.93; at night `--paper` 1.89, `--paper-2` 1.65. It also fails on the chrome it was drawn for, at the light end of the gradients: `--rail-hi` 2.91 and `--pine-hi` 2.35, while `--rail-lo` passes at 5.16 — a ring that appears and disappears along one surface. The decisive number is the RulerBar, whose background is `--grad-brass` itself (`components/chrome/RulerBar.jsx:7`): a brass ring measures **1.86:1 at the bar's light stop and 1.00:1 at its dark stop**. On the one strip the design system builds out of brass, the brass ring is not there.

**Flat `--accent` fails the mirror image of that.** Under bench `--accent` is `--pine` #39543C: 7.76 on `--paper`, 6.42 on `--wall-lo`, 7.97 on night `--paper` — the ADR-074 rule is excellent everywhere it has ever been measured. On chrome it collapses: `--rail-hi` 1.11, `--rail-mid` 1.31, `--pine-hi` 1.38, and **1.00 on `--pine`**, a pine ring around a pine button. The 2px offset does not rescue it, because the gap shows the rail, not the sheet.

**Decision.** The ring is two layers, one global rule, every surface:

```
:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--brass-edge); outline-offset: 2px;
                 box-shadow: 0 0 0 5px var(--focus-halo); }
```

where `--focus-halo` is the cream `rgba(238, 217, 163, .85)` — a fixed value in both themes, deliberately not swapped with `--brass-hi`, because the halo's job is to be the lighter of the two layers regardless of what the theme does to the metal. This is not invention: `cedarclerk-web/src/styles/_forest.scss:419-423` already ships it, and ADR-136 deletes that partial. It folds into the base `:focus-visible` block rather than dying with it — one of the four things ADR-136 lists as having to be lifted out before the file goes. `.tiptap` keeps its exclusion unchanged — the caret is the indicator on the sheet.

What the two layers buy, measured against every bench surface a control can sit on, in both themes (paper, paper-2, paper-bright, paper-edge, wall-hi, wall-lo, leaf-bg-2, rail-hi, rail-mid, rail-lo, sign-tile-hi, sign-tile-lo, pine, pine-hi, wood-hi, plus their night values, plus the cork panel and both stops of `--grad-brass`):

- The dark outline against the cream halo is a **constant** boundary inside the band, independent of what is underneath: **worst 4.21:1** (day `--rail-lo`), best 6.91.
- On light surfaces the outline additionally clears the surface itself: **4.82–7.44** day, **4.60–7.59** night.
- On dark surfaces the halo carries it: **3.62–7.38** day (worst `--pine-hi`), **4.69–9.06** night.

**Rejected: darken brass until it measures.** Brass dark enough for paper is `--brass-lo` #8A6226 — 5.05 on `--paper`, 4.18 on `--wall-lo`, but 1.38 on `--rail-hi`, 2.02 on `--rail-mid`, 1.11 on `--pine-hi`. Darkening trades one family of surfaces for the other; there is no stop on that line that clears both. It also costs the token its identity: #8A6226 measures 1.43:1 against `--wood-groove`, so a brass visible on paper is a brass indistinguishable from the wood it rings.

**Rejected: bind `--focus-ring` to the surface class** — `--accent` under `data-surface="paper"` (worst 5.32), `--brass-hi` under `data-surface="chrome"` (worst 4.36). Both halves measure, and ADR-138 already ships the attribute. It is rejected because it makes the accessibility floor depend on every component remembering to declare its surface, and the failure is silent: a missing attribute is an invisible focus ring, not a red test. The second reason is decisive on its own — `--accent` is user-chosen and injected at `:root` by `core/appearance.service.ts:124`, so half the ring's contrast would be a value the Appearance panel hands to the user. The ring is the floor; it must not be an input.

**Consequence.** The ring is the one place the app deliberately spends a `box-shadow`: a focused control loses its own shadow for as long as it is focused. That reads as a glow rather than a defect, and it is why the halo is not a second outline.

One measured exception is accepted with eyes open: on `--wood-hi` #B68B60 — the light stop of `--shelf-frame` — neither boundary against the surface clears the floor (outline 2.47, halo 1.98). What carries the ring there is the 4.90:1 boundary inside the band. Nothing focusable is placed on a bare wood frame today; if something is, that surface needs its own answer. On the ruler the halo disappears into the brass (1.00 at the light stop) and the dark outline is the whole indicator, at 5.45 there and 2.94 at the dark stop.

Nothing automated checks this. `check-contrast.mjs` scores flat token against flat token (`SURFACES`, `check-contrast.mjs:89`); the ring's backdrops are gradients and its halo is an alpha composite, so the checker cannot see the pair at all, and `e2e/12-a11y.spec.ts` asserts the contrast run and accessible names, never the ring. The Stage 0 checker rework — composite alpha, evaluate gradients at every stop — is what brings these numbers under CI. Until then this entry is the record, and the numbers here are the ones to re-derive against if a surface moves.

`--brass-edge` and `--focus-halo` are bench material names, not contract names — ADR-137's additive half — so `DesignTokenDriftTests` does not pin them and they are free to move when a measurement moves them. The mirror's `--focus-ring`, `--focus-outline` and `--focus-offset` are not ported — a single-value ring token is the shape of the defect. `components/forms/Input.jsx:8`'s focus treatment (`border-color: var(--brass)` plus a 25%-alpha brass wash) is not ported either: at 2.39:1 that is a decorative highlight wearing an indicator's job.

Supersedes the focus clause of ADR-074 as stated in `DESIGN.md` §Accessibility. The `.tiptap` exclusion survives; the `--accent` colour does not.
