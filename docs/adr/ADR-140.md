# ADR-140 — Focus ring: two layers, because no single colour clears 3:1 on both paper and rail

**Context.** The mirror prescribes one flat ring: `--focus-outline: 2px solid var(--focus-ring)` at `--focus-offset: 2px` (`.design-sync/ds-v2/tokens/materials.css:27-28`) with `--focus-ring: var(--brass)` (`tokens/colors.css:71`). The app prescribes a different flat ring: one global `:focus-visible` of `2px solid var(--accent)` at 2px offset (`cedarclerk-web/src/styles.scss:269-272`), with `.tiptap` the sole exclusion (`styles.scss:277`) — ADR-074, restated at `docs/design/DESIGN.md:142`. Bench becoming the only look forces a choice between them, and it also changes the question: bench is the first look in which controls sit on *dark* chrome while the theme is light. A ring measured only against paper has never been the whole test.

Ratios below use the sRGB luminance formula of `cedarclerk-web/tools/check-contrast.mjs`, with alpha composited over the backdrop rather than discarded and gradients read at their end stops. Both are the checker's own work, and it does the second one further than this entry does: it walks the whole ramp between the stops. A figure below that names a stop is that stop's ratio, not necessarily the ramp's worst. The floor is 3:1, SC 1.4.11.

**Flat `--brass` #C69A4E fails on every reading surface in both themes** — `--paper` 2.39, `--paper-2` 2.15, `--paper-bright` 2.53, `--wall-hi` 2.21, `--wall-lo` 1.98, `--leaf-bg` 1.93; at night `--paper` 1.89, `--paper-2` 1.65. It also fails on the chrome it was drawn for, at the light end of the gradients: `--rail-hi` 2.91 and `--pine-hi` 2.35, while `--rail-lo` passes at 5.16 — a ring that appears and disappears along one surface. The decisive number is the RulerBar, whose background is `--grad-brass` itself (`components/chrome/RulerBar.jsx:7`): a brass ring measures **1.86:1 at the bar's light stop and 1.00:1 at its dark stop**. On the one strip the design system builds out of brass, the brass ring is not there.

**Flat `--accent` fails the mirror image of that.** Under bench `--accent` is `--pine` #39543C: 7.76 on `--paper`, 6.42 on `--wall-lo`, 6.13 on night `--paper` — the ADR-074 rule is excellent everywhere it has ever been measured. On chrome it collapses: `--rail-hi` 1.11, `--rail-mid` 1.31, `--pine-hi` 1.38, and **1.00 on `--pine`**, a pine ring around a pine button. The 2px offset does not rescue it, because the gap shows the rail, not the sheet.

**Decision.** The ring is two layers, one global rule, every surface:

```
:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--brass-edge); outline-offset: 2px;
                 box-shadow: 0 0 0 5px var(--focus-halo); }
```

where `--focus-halo` is the cream `rgba(238, 217, 163, .85)` — a fixed value in both themes, deliberately not swapped with `--brass-hi`, because the halo's job is to be the lighter of the two layers regardless of what the theme does to the metal. This is not invention: `cedarclerk-web/src/styles/_forest.scss:419-423` already ships it, and ADR-136 deletes that partial. It folds into the base `:focus-visible` block rather than dying with it — one of the four things ADR-136 lists as having to be lifted out before the file goes. `.tiptap` keeps its exclusion, and it has to clear both layers: `outline: none` on its own leaves a cream halo drawn around the sheet you are writing on. The caret is the indicator there.

What the two layers buy, measured against every bench surface a control can sit on, in both themes (paper, paper-2, paper-bright, paper-edge, wall-hi, wall-lo, leaf-bg-2, rail-hi, rail-mid, rail-lo, sign-tile-hi, sign-tile-lo, pine, pine-hi, wood-hi, plus their night values, plus the cork panel and both stops of `--grad-brass`):

- The dark outline against the cream halo is a **constant** boundary inside the band, independent of what is underneath: **worst 4.20:1** (day `--rail-lo`), best 6.91 (night `--paper-bright`).
- On light surfaces the outline additionally clears the surface itself: **4.82–7.44** day, **4.60–7.59** night.
- On dark surfaces the halo carries it: **4.00–7.38** day, **4.10–9.09** night — worst `--pine-hi` in both, best `--rail-lo` in both.

**Rejected: darken brass until it measures.** Brass dark enough for paper is `--brass-lo` #8A6226 — 5.05 on `--paper`, 4.18 on `--wall-lo`, but 1.38 on `--rail-hi`, 2.02 on `--rail-mid`, 1.11 on `--pine-hi`. Darkening trades one family of surfaces for the other; there is no stop on that line that clears both. It also costs the token its identity: #8A6226 measures 1.43:1 against `--wood-groove`, so a brass visible on paper is a brass indistinguishable from the wood it rings.

**Rejected: bind `--focus-ring` to the surface class** — `--accent` under `data-surface="paper"` (worst 4.07, night `--paper-edge` #C2B48D; the day half of that family bottoms out at 5.32), `--brass-hi` under `data-surface="chrome"` (worst 4.36). Both halves measure, and ADR-138 already ships the attribute. It is rejected because it makes the accessibility floor depend on every component remembering to declare its surface, and the failure is silent: a missing attribute is an invisible focus ring, not a red test. The second reason is decisive on its own — `--accent` is user-chosen and injected at `:root` by `core/appearance.service.ts:124`, so half the ring's contrast would be a value the Appearance panel hands to the user. The ring is the floor; it must not be an input.

**Consequence.** The ring is the one place the app deliberately spends a `box-shadow`: a focused control loses its own shadow for as long as it is focused. That reads as a glow rather than a defect, and it is why the halo is not a second outline.

Three measured cases are accepted with eyes open. On `--wood-hi` #B68B60 — the light stop of `--shelf-frame` — neither boundary against the surface clears the floor (outline 2.47, halo 1.98). What carries the ring there is the 4.90:1 boundary inside the band. Nothing focusable is placed on a bare wood frame today; if something is, that surface needs its own answer. The second is cork — `ShelfPanel` with `tone="cork"` paints a flat #C9A46B (`.design-sync/ds-v2/components/chrome/ShelfPanel.jsx:27`), and it belongs to neither family above. The outline against it is 3.25 day and 4.05 night, over the floor but under the light-surface range, while the halo barely parts from it at 1.56 in both themes. The band carries it, 5.07 day and 6.31 night. Cork is a hex inside a component rather than a token, so no pair in the checker can name it. The third is the ruler: the halo disappears into the brass (1.00 at the light stop) and the dark outline is the whole indicator, at 5.45 there and 2.94 at the dark stop.

The ring is checked, not remembered. `check-contrast.mjs` composites alpha over the backdrop a pair is measured against and walks a gradient along its whole ramp — sixteen steps between adjacent stops, no interpolation across a hard edge where two stops meet at one position, and a bisection to the exact colour when the ink passes through the surface's own luminance, which is worth 1.00 and is a colour no stop names. That is the shape this ring needs — a translucent halo over gradient chrome — and its table carries both layers against every surface a focusable control sits on, plus the boundary inside the band, measured per surface because the halo is only as light as what it lies on. The wood frame and the ruler are carried there as named exceptions, each with its reason attached, rather than as surfaces quietly left out of the list. What no pair can reach is a surface that is not a token: cork is a hex inside a mirror component, so the numbers for it live here and nowhere else. `e2e/12-a11y.spec.ts` asserts the contrast run and accessible names, never the ring as rendered. The numbers here are the ones to re-derive against if a surface moves.

`--brass-edge` and `--focus-halo` are bench material names, not contract names — ADR-137's additive half — so `DesignTokenDriftTests` does not pin them and they are free to move when a measurement moves them. The mirror's `--focus-ring`, `--focus-outline` and `--focus-offset` are not ported — a single-value ring token is the shape of the defect. `components/forms/Input.jsx:8`'s focus treatment (`border-color: var(--brass)` plus a 25%-alpha brass wash) is not ported either: at 2.39:1 that is a decorative highlight wearing an indicator's job.

Supersedes the focus clause of ADR-074 as stated in `DESIGN.md` §Accessibility. The `.tiptap` exclusion survives; the `--accent` colour does not.
