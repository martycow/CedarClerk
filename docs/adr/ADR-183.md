# ADR-183 — The overflow menu hangs on the tool wall, not on the sign board (22.08.2026)

**Context.** ADR-151 put the theme toggle and the Appearance trigger behind the `dots` button in
`RailHeader`'s right slot, and ADR-139 clause 4 had already spent that control on Glossary, Admin
and the two `/dev/*` screens. Its own attribution paragraph says it plainly: the placement was the
implementer's default, shown to Marty rather than asked of him, and cheaply reversible — "one entry
moved out of one menu".

Marty has now asked the question that entry deferred, and answered it: *Glossary, Appearance and
the other buttons should be on the side bar.* The answer is not one entry. It is the menu.

Two things make the sign board the wrong home for it. Four of the six entries are **screens**, and
the wall down the left edge is where this app puts screens — a hook per destination, the current one
marked three ways. A screen reachable only from a menu on the opposite side of the header is a
screen the wall is lying about. And the right slot is crowded: it holds the save state, the page's
one primary action and the account plaque, so the dots button stands between the thing a page is
telling you and the thing you press. That crowding is what produced the second defect in the same
week — the panel and the account popover, siblings in one header, each staying open under the other.

`HookRail.prompt.md`'s own budget is what stops the simple answer. The wall holds seven hooks,
because "a wall with everything on it is a wall you stop reading", and six are spent. Glossary alone
fits; Glossary plus Appearance plus Admin plus two dev screens does not, and Appearance is not a
screen at all — it is a modal, and a hook that opens a dialog would be the wall lying the other way.

**Decision.**

1. **`HookRail` grows a tray: one `dots` control pinned at the foot of the wall, under the tail
   hook, opening a paper panel to the right of the pegboard.** It is outside the `<ul>`, so the
   navigation landmark still announces the hooks and only the hooks, and the budget is unchanged —
   the tray is not a hook and does not count against seven.

2. **`RailHeader` loses its menu entirely** — the button, the panel, the slot, the open state and
   the six specs that measured them. Not kept as unused library surface: two components able to draw
   the same menu is the thing `.claude/rules/ui-changes.md` rule 1 exists to prevent, and the mirror
   names a `dots` button without naming which side of the screen it lives on.

3. **The tray's behaviour is the menu-button pattern the header's was**, moved with it: Escape
   closes and returns focus to the trigger, an outside click closes, an entry that navigates takes
   the panel with it and one that acts in place — theme, Appearance — leaves it standing. The
   trigger, and only the trigger, holds it open against a document click, which is the fix the
   sibling-popover defect needed.

**Rejected.** *Glossary as a seventh hook, menu stays on the header.* It spends the wall's whole
remaining budget on one screen and leaves the menu exactly where Marty said it should not be.
*Splitting: screens to the wall, theme and Appearance to the header.* Two homes for one overflow,
and the header keeps a button for two entries — the shape ADR-095 had to be written to undo.
*A tray that is just another hook.* A hook is a link to a screen; this one opens a panel in place,
and drawing it identically would make the wall's one grammar mean two things.

**Consequence.** Everything that is not one of the six screens now lives at one address, and that
address is on the side the user already looks at to change screens. `RailHeader`'s right slot goes
back to what `RailHeader.prompt.md` reserved it for — save state, the primary action, the account —
and the two popovers that used to overlap are no longer siblings. ADR-151 clauses 2 and 3 stand
unchanged: the Appearance panel is still a child of the shell, and the pre-auth pages still carry
their own toggle. Only clause 1's *where* is superseded.
