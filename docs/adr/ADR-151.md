# ADR-151 — The dots menu takes the rare rest: theme and Appearance leave the header

**Context.** Two controls lose their home in Stage 3 and no bench surface names a replacement.

The theme toggle ships in five templates: `shared/page-header.component.html:52`, which is twelve authenticated screens; the editor topbar at `pages/editor.component.html:73`; and `login`, `register` and `legal-page`, which have no shared header at all. `T-218` deletes the first two. The Appearance panel is worse off — its **only** trigger anywhere in the app is the palette button in that same editor topbar (`editor.component.html:69`), and the component itself is instantiated in exactly one template, `editor.component.html:1391`. Delete the topbar nav block and the panel still exists, reachable by nothing.

The design system does define a slot for this. `.design-sync/ds-v2/components/chrome/RailHeader.prompt.md` explains why V2 has no desktop menu bar — "fake furniture on a web app: it cost 34px on every screen and hid commands one level deep" — and says where what it carried went: the project switcher became a carved tile in the rail, per-panel commands moved into their `ShelfPanel` header, "and the rare rest live behind one `dots` button in the right slot". ADR-139 clause 4 already spends that control on Glossary, Admin and `/dev/*`, and clause 5 puts the account menu beside it.

**Decision.** The theme toggle and the Appearance trigger go into the `RailHeader` dots menu.

**Attribution.** *This is the implementer's default, taken under Marty's general instruction to proceed with the port. It was shown to him as a defaulted choice rather than asked as a question, and he did not object — that is not the same as his answering it, and this entry must not be read as his decision.* It is cheaply reversible: both are entries in one menu, and moving either onto the rail proper is markup, not architecture.

1. **Both controls go behind the one `dots` button** the prompt file names, in the rail's right slot, beside the destinations ADR-139 already put there. Nothing new is added to the rail's visible row.

2. **The Appearance panel is hoisted into the shell.** It stops being a child of `editor.component` and becomes a child of `bench-shell.component`, because a trigger in shared chrome cannot open a modal parented to one page.

3. **The pre-auth pages keep their own toggle.** `/login`, `/register`, `/terms` and `/privacy` sit outside the shell by ADR-139 clause 2 and have no rail to hang anything on. Their `.theme-toggle` is not a second home for a control that has one — it is the only home on a page with no chrome.

**What was rejected.** *A theme toggle on the rail itself.* It is the honest placement for how often theme is used, and it is a control the design system does not describe — inventing a rail affordance in the first stage that ports the rail is how a system stops being one. *Sending Appearance to `/settings` instead.* Tempting, since `.claude/rules/ui-changes.md` rule 1 asks whether a home already exists, and `/settings` has sections. It was rejected because the panel is a live-preview modal whose sheet-width, typeface, ruler and paragraph-number controls act on the editor's sheet; a settings section makes those controls judge themselves on a screen that does not show what they change — the failure the panel's own history already records once, when it moved from a pinned column into a modal. *Deleting the theme toggle and following the OS.* `ThemeService` already reads `prefers-color-scheme` for a first visit and persists an explicit choice under `cedar-theme`; removing the choice would break a user who wants dark chrome on a light desktop, for one control's worth of tidiness.

**Consequence, and its cost.** Theme is not rare, and the prompt file's slot is for the rare. A one-click toggle in a header becomes two clicks behind a menu on every screen, and that is the price of not inventing a surface. If it turns out to be the wrong price, the fix is one entry moved out of one menu — which is the reason this was defaulted rather than escalated.

The gain runs the other way for Appearance: it is editor-only today, even though theme and accent are global and apply to every screen. Behind the rail it is reachable from all of them for the first time. The matching risk is stated with it — opened on a screen with no sheet, roughly half its controls act on something the user cannot see. Whether that half moves out later is a question for whoever ports `/settings`, not for this entry.

`e2e` binds `.theme-toggle` in the smoke suite; `T-233` already owns rebinding those selectors to the bench markup and this adds one more to its list. The inventory rows for both controls are rewritten by the commit that lands `T-216`/`T-217`, not by this one.
