# ADR-175 — The rail face is a tint that needs wood under it: the doors' theme toggle is a paper button at the chrome box (22.08.2026)

**Context.** ADR-166 clause 2 keeps a theme toggle on each of the four doors outside the shell and
says what it is made of: *"a `rail`-variant button, so it is chrome sitting on the wall rather than a
control on the paper."* That held while the rail variant's face was an opaque wood. The fidelity
pass put the kit's own value on it — `--rail-btn-face: rgba(0, 0, 0, .16)`, a translucent tint — and
the toggle on the day wall became a grey square with a cream moon on it, measured at about 1.5:1
(the tester's R1). Night was fine, because the night wall is dark enough to carry cream.

Nothing caught it before a person looked. The pair table measures the rail face under
`--surface-rail` and `--grad-sign-tile`, the two woods the rail variant is placed on inside the
shell; the wall is not among them, so the combination was never scored. The a11y smoke visits
`/drafts`, `/posts`, `/settings` and `/editor`, not `/login`. And the variant's own rule is right as
far as it goes: on the rail the tint darkens wood that already carries cream, and the pair passes.

**Decision.** The doors' toggle is `variant="paper" surface="chrome"`, and the rail variant is
painted on wood or not at all.

1. **A paper face at the chrome box.** The `surface` input exists for exactly this placement — a
   control whose box is decided by where it stands rather than by its variant (the pine button on the
   rail is the other case). The toggle keeps the 30px box and `--text-chrome` type of a chrome
   control, because it stands alone on the wall among no paper, and takes the paper face with
   `--text` as its ink, which is measured on every paper in both themes.

2. **The rail variant's face is a tint, and a tint's contrast belongs to what is under it.** The
   pair table scores it under the rail and the sign tile, which is where it may be placed. A rail
   button on any other ground is unmeasured, and the doors were the one place that had one.

3. **ADR-166 clause 2 is superseded in its second sentence only.** The toggle stays, it is still the
   page's own, and it is still chrome by box; what changes is that "chrome sitting on the wall"
   names the box, not the paint. The first sentence of the clause and clauses 1, 3, 4 and 5 stand.

**Rejected.** *Adding wall pairs for the rail face.* They fail by day and cannot be made to pass
without a second, opaque face token — a rail button that looks different off the rail is a paper
button with a misleading name. *Keeping the rail variant and darkening the day wall.* The wall is
the kit's value and the ground of four screens; moving it for one control is the wrong end.

**Consequence.** By day the toggle is paper ink on a paper face; at night nothing visible changed.
`docs/design/UI-INVENTORY.md`'s two rows for the doors say `paper`/`chrome` now. The gap that let
it through is the real cost: a control on a surface the pair table does not list is not measured,
and the doors are the only screens with a wall under a control. The a11y smoke still does not visit
them.
