# ADR-223 — Steam and itch.io are copy targets: renderers, never automation (29.08.2026)

**Context.** Steam and itch.io are the two channels where an indie's players actually live, and the export rack drew both as placeholder connector cards with nothing behind them (T-318). The Wave 1 competitor research and T-127's API half agree on the ground truth: Steam has no public posting API — announcements go through the partner site, and every third-party tool that "automates" it does so by puppeteering a logged-in session against the Steamworks ToS; itch.io exposes no devlog write endpoint at all. The account such automation risks is not a throwaway — it is the account that owns the game. Meanwhile "one document, many renderers" already turns one CedarJson into every other output.

**Decision.** Steam and itch.io become **copy targets**, not `IPublishTarget` implementations:

1. `CedarToSteamBbcodeRenderer` and `CedarToItchHtmlRenderer` live in Core under the standard renderer invariants — user text always escaped, a unit test for every mark and block (86 between them), plus each target's own hazards: `[noparse]` breakout protection in BBCode, a URL scheme allowlist in both.
2. `GET /api/drafts/{id}/export-text` serves the rendered output; the export modal's Steam and itch cards show a rendered preview and a clipboard button. No connector, no stored credentials, nothing to link in Settings.
3. **Never automation.** No session puppeteering, no scraping, no "unofficial API" — even if a partner API becomes reachable, the clipboard path stays until an official write API with acceptable terms exists, and switching would be a new ADR.

**Consequence.** Publishing to a storefront is one paste instead of one click, which is the deliberate price: the author's Steam and itch accounts are never at risk from this app. The rack's Steam and itch cards stop being placeholders (T-318's remaining question narrows to Instagram/Threads/YouTube), and the renderers extend the architecture rather than adding a mechanism. What the choice forgoes: scheduled sends to these channels, receipts, and per-post stats — none of which an unofficial path could deliver honestly anyway.
