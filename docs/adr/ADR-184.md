# ADR-184 — The bench's bottom edge is wood, not a brass rule (22.08.2026)

**Context.** `RulerBar` was ported as the kit draws it: a brass carpenter's rule with milled ticks —
two repeating gradients, one every 12px and one every 72 — under mono numerals in `--brass-ink`. It
is the only surface in the app painted that way, and it sits directly under the drawer lip, which is
wood.

Marty's reading of it, unprompted: *I don't really like this ruler. It looks odd.* Asked what to do
with it, he chose a wooden plank.

He is describing something the port could have measured and did not. Every other piece of chrome in
the shell is one of two materials — wood for structure (rail, pegboard, drawer lip, sign tiles),
brass for hardware (hooks, pulls, the current-tool bar, the account plaque). Brass is what you grip
or what points at something; the ticked rule grips nothing and points at nothing. It is a whole
surface wearing hardware, and the eye reads a milled scale as a thing to measure with, on a strip
that only ever prints words. That is why it reads as a prop dropped onto the screen.

**Decision.**

1. **The rule takes the shell's wood.** `--surface-rail` under `--tex-wood`, a `--rail-edge`
   hairline along its top and the lit inset the rail already carries, with `--rail-ink` on it. The
   two tick gradients and the `--tick`/`--tick-major` spacings go; nothing else measured them and
   no token family named them.

2. **It stays distinguishable from the drawer lip above it.** The lip is a drawer front — brass
   pull, hover, a cursor — and the rule is the bench's cut edge: no pull, no hover, no pointer, and
   the wood is laid the darker way round so the two boards read as two boards. The rule against a
   control is unchanged and is what the split protects: *a slot here is an invitation to put a
   control in it, and the invitation is the violation.*

3. **The numerals keep their face.** `--font-readout` at `--text-chrome-sm` with tabular figures:
   the reason they are mono is that a count which ticks must not shuffle the bar (ADR-180), and that
   reason has nothing to do with what the strip is made of.

**Rejected.** *Keeping the brass and dropping only the ticks.* Brass with no milling is the account
plaque's face spread across the full width of the screen — the metal was never the complaint's
subject on its own, but a metal band under a wooden bench is the same wrong note more quietly.
*Deleting the strip.* The readouts have nowhere else to go: the worktop's chalk edge belongs to the
page and the drawer belongs to the journal, and the save word in particular has to be visible on
every screen. *Paper.* Paper on the outer edge of the chrome would be a sheet under the tool wall,
which is the one direction the surface split (ADR-138) does not run.

**Consequence.** The bottom edge stops being the only brass surface in the app, and the two
materials go back to meaning what they mean elsewhere: wood is what the bench is made of, brass is
what you take hold of. The ink pair changes with the ground — `--rail-ink` on `--surface-rail` is
already the scored pair the brand and crumbs use, so the strip joins a measurement instead of
carrying its own. ADR-180's readout face and its 11px grid are untouched.
