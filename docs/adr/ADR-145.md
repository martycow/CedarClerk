# ADR-145 — A wash is a token: state tints, and inks with room to sit on them

**Context.** `tools/check-contrast.mjs` scored a hand-written pair list and reported zero failures over three rounds. A census of what `cedarclerk-web/src/app` actually paints found the list measuring the wrong things: `--accent` appears in 189 declarations, `--sheet` in 163, `--danger` in 116, while `--bg`, `--canvas`, `--wood-ink`, `--pegboard` and `--grad-sign-tile` appear in none — and the pairs covered the second group. The combinations that carry every screen were absent, and three of them fail.

They fail for one reason, in one shape. A state badge is its own ink on a tint of itself, and the tint was never a token: twenty-two rules each wrote `color-mix(in srgb, var(--danger) N%, <whatever paper this component sits on>)` with `N` between 7 and 18 and three different papers, so one visual idea shipped as twenty-two ratios and the table could name none of them. Measured, they run 3.63–4.46 against a 4.5 floor — `.chip.warn` in `admin.component.css` at 3.63 in night is the worst, and `admin.component.html` prints `lapsedChip`, `notUsable` and `private` into it.

The tints only fail because the inks have nowhere to stand. ADR-141 derived night's values against bare `--surface` and stopped there, so each one clears 4.5:1 on paper and nothing else: `--t2` measures 4.98, `--ok` 4.52, `--warn` 4.56. Any wash drawn under them — the accent wash `--asoft`, the hover wash `--hover-strong`, their own tint — spends the whole remainder. That is why `--t2` on `--asoft` measures 4.26–4.36 at night across the five accent presets in `ACCENT_PRESETS` while `--t2` on `--surface` passes: the pair that passes is not the pair the app paints. Real sites are `.hint-bubble` in the editor, glossary and posts manager, `.notice` in project assets, and `.sep-label` on the blog (`CedarClerk.Server/BlogEndpoints.cs`).

Two smaller findings share the shape. `--t3` — barred from carrying content by ADR-074, and the base block says so — is the only label of `.lang-code`, a `<button>` reading RU or EN, and of `.column-empty`, a sentence. And `.pr-part` in the publish progress bar sets `color: var(--alt)` on `background: var(--sheet)`: paper as ink on paper, 1.06:1, a number nobody can read in either theme.

**Decision.**

1. **Three wash tokens beside `--asoft`, on the same formula.** `--ok-soft`, `--warn-soft` and `--danger-soft` mix their ink into `--surface` at 13% by day and 10% at night, which is what `--asoft` already does and why. Mixing into the deepest paper rather than into whatever the component sits on makes the wash one flat colour everywhere it lands, so the ink on it is one pair rather than a family of near-misses. Every ad-hoc state tint in the app is replaced by the token; the 30–45% border mixes beside them stay as they are, being the decorative hairlines ADR-074 deliberately leaves unmeasured.

2. **An ink is derived against the wash it is painted on, not against bare paper.** ADR-141's method is unchanged and its floor is unchanged — this is the same derivation carried one surface further, to where the ink actually lands. Night takes its step down and day keeps its hue and takes the smallest step that clears (ADR-074): night `--t2` `#5C5138`→`#564C35`, `--ok` `#33623F`→`#2D5738`, `--warn` `#74511E`→`#68491B`, `--danger` `#8F3520`→`#85311E`; day `--ok` `#386D46`→`#356842` and `--danger` `#A84129`→`#9E3D27`, with `--warn` already clearing. The cost is `--t2`'s separation from `--text` at night, 2.12:1 down to 1.95:1, which is where day already sits.

3. **`--t3` is not a label, and `--alt` is not an ink.** `.lang-code`, `.column-count` and `.column-empty` take `--t2`; `.pr-part` takes `--t2`. A control whose only label fails its floor is a defect in the rule — the floor is not the thing to move, and neither is the token's job description.

4. **The hover washes are measured on all three papers, and what rides them decides the floor.** `--hover`, `--hover-strong` and `--hover-danger` are translucent so a hovered row works on whatever paper it lies on, so each pair states its `under`. Every rule that puts `--t2` on `--hover-strong` is a square icon button, and a glyph owes WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 — but `page-header`'s `.nav-btn` was a *labelled* button on that wash, and it takes `--text`. A label on an icon's floor is the one thing this clause exists to catch.

5. **Chrome glass is measured twice and fixed neither time.** `--glass` is 80% `--surface`, painted by `page-header` and the editor topbar over whatever scrolls beneath. Over `--surface`, which is what `body` paints and so the case nine tenths of the time, `--t2` measures 6.51 by day and 5.40 at night and is a pair like any other. Over `--text`, the darkest fill the app itself paints, it measures 4.43 and 3.69, and `--t3` on `.crumb-sep` measures 2.23 and 2.06 against 3:1. Those two are recorded as **accepted exceptions carrying their reason**, not repaired: the true backdrop is the document, a photograph under the bar can be any colour, and no opacity of a translucent surface answers that. Raising `--glass` to 95% would close `--t2` and still miss `--t3`, and it would close them by deleting the material ADR-035 chose. ADR-139 stage 3 deletes both surfaces and the material with them.

**What was rejected.** *Weakening the washes instead of the inks* — a 8% tint clears the floor by day with no ink change at all, and fails at every percentage at night, because night's problem is the ink's headroom and not the tint's strength. *Lightening night's `--hover-strong`* on the theory that a wash bites harder in darker paper — measured, the step from paper to hover wash is 1.272:1 at night against 1.276:1 by day, so the theory is false and the fix would have made hovers invisible for nothing. *Patching the eight failing rules* leaves the formula in twenty-two places and the next badge picks a twenty-third percentage.

**Consequence.** The pair list gains the combinations the app paints — the three state washes, `--text` and `--t2` on `--asoft`, the three hover washes on all three papers, and glass in both its backdrops — and the census that found them stays as a second mode of the same tool, so a wash invented next month is reported rather than assumed. Two colour literals painted next to tokens (`#F4F2EA` on `--accent` in `count-badge`, and again on `--danger` in the annotation control) become `--sheet`, which already carries both pairs; the brand fills in `settings.component.css` stay literals, being other people's colours. `styles/_forest.scss` still reports twenty-five census failures and none of them is real — no rule in it can match, since nothing sets `data-skin` — and that is the price of keeping a neutralised file measured rather than excluded.
