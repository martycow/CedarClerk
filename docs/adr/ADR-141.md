# ADR-141 — Night is re-derived against cream paper, not ported

**Context.** Cedar Bench becomes the only look (ADR-136), so its values land on the bare `:root` (`cedarclerk-web/src/styles.scss:9`) and on `:root[data-theme="dark"]` (`styles.scss:178`) — the contract ADR-137 defines, read by `tools/check-contrast.mjs` and emitted into `DesignTokens` for the blog and the landing page. The mirror's night block (`.design-sync/ds-v2/tokens/colors.css:75`, `tokens/bench.css:45`) is written as an ordinary dark theme: every value travels towards white. But the same block keeps the night reading surface **cream** — `--paper: #E6DCC2`, `--paper-2: #D9CEAE` (`colors.css:88-89`). Those two habits contradict each other, and the contradiction is measurable from the mirror's own hexes:

- `--text-body` resolves to `--ink`, which night sets to `#241E13` (`colors.css:64`, `:92`). Every kit card renders `body { color: var(--text-body); background: var(--surface-page) }` (`components/*/*.card.html:8`), and `--surface-page` is the wall gradient (`colors.css:57`). Night: **1.12:1** on `--wall-hi`, **1.01:1** on `--wall-lo`. Body text is not dim, it is absent. The app's own `body` rule is the same shape — `styles.scss:341-346`.
- `GrowthChart` draws on `--surface-card` → `--paper` (`GrowthChart.jsx:71`). Night series against it: pine `#7C9A72` **2.29**, ink `#7FA2C4` **1.95**, resin `#D2A055` **1.73**, moss `#9AAE72` **1.77** (`bench.css:48-51`), against a 3:1 floor. On the deeper cream — which is what `check-contrast.mjs:106` actually pairs a series against — 1.99 / 1.70 / 1.50 / 1.54.
- `--rust` lightens `#A84129` → `#B65138` (`colors.css:105`): **3.17** as a label on the deeper cream, **3.64** under cream text as a button fill.

The correct answer already exists in this repository. `styles/_forest.scss:91-159` derives night downward and says why at `:102-103` and `:109`; those values clear their floors. What follows is that rule, promoted from a partial nothing could check into the contract everything checks.

**Decision.**

1. **At night the direction of travel is down, not up.** Night's reading surfaces are cream, so night text, status and series values are *darker* than day's. This is ADR-074's light-theme rule applied to a light surface that happens to be lit by a lamp — the name of the theme is not evidence about the polarity of its surfaces.
2. **The binding surface is the deepest cream**, `--surface` `#D9CEAE`. Dark-on-light contrast falls as the ground darkens, which is the same reason ADR-074 measured against `--canvas` and not `--sheet`.
3. **Floors, by class of token, all on `--surface`:** `--text --t2 --accent --danger --ok --warn` ≥ 4.5:1; `--t3` and `--border-strong` ≥ 3:1, with `--t3` still barred from carrying content (ADR-074); `--series-1..6` ≥ 3:1; and `--sheet` over an `--accent` or `--danger` fill ≥ 4.5:1. The forest night set is the starting point rather than a fresh derivation: re-measured against `#D9CEAE`, every one of its values clears the floor above — text 10.54, `--t2` 4.98, accent 5.34, danger 4.96, ok 4.52, warn 4.56, `--border-strong` 3.54, the six series 3.02–3.39, `--sheet` on accent 6.13 and on danger 5.70 — with exactly one exception. `--t3` `#83765A` measures **2.85** and moves to roughly `#7F7257` (3.01). That defect was invisible only because the checker never read the partial.
4. **Night carries two ink polarities, and each has its own token.** `--text/--t2/--t3` are paper ink and are measured on cream. The wall (`--bg`, `--canvas`) is dark, and text over it uses the mirror's own `--wood-ink` — `#3B2A18` by day (10.52 on `--wall-lo`), `#E3D3AE` at night (11.05). Consequently the text pairs in `check-contrast.mjs:89` no longer run `--text` against `--bg`/`--canvas`: no single value clears 4.5:1 on both a dark wall and cream paper, so a pair that demands it is not a standard, it is an unsatisfiable assertion. Those two surfaces pair against `--wood-ink` instead.
5. **`SUGGEST` picks its direction from the background, not from the theme name.** `check-contrast.mjs:135` currently moves towards white for anything in the dark theme, so for every failing night pair it would propose exactly the move that produced the 1.01:1 above.
6. **The injected user accent is re-derived with night.** ADR-120 kept a saved preset from repainting night pine by specificity: `:root[data-skin="forest"][data-theme="dark"]` outranked the `:root[data-theme="dark"]` rule written by `core/appearance.service.ts:125`. With the skin retired the two selectors are equal and the injected `<style>` comes later in `<head>`, so the preset wins — the reversal ADR-136 records as a consequence, priced here. Its dark branch is `color-mix(in srgb, <accentDark> 55%, #E8F0E8 45%)` — a lightening mix built for dark ground; on the binding surface `--surface` `#D9CEAE` the five shipped presets land at **1.53–2.23:1** (Bark 1.53, Cedar 1.59, Rust 1.65, Slate 1.76, Ink 2.23; on the lighter `--sheet` they reach 2.56 and no further). The dark branch therefore mixes towards the ink, and every preset in `ACCENT_PRESETS` is held to the same 4.5:1 as the default. A preset that cannot clear it stops being a preset.

**What was rejected.** *Darkening night's paper along with the wall* removes every rule above at a stroke and is one edit away — rejected because the lamp-lit sheet is the idea the look is built on, and it is the single thing V1 and V2 agree about. *Porting night as authored and repairing screens as they are noticed* is how forest ended up hand-listing forty selectors (`_forest.scss:1141-1243`) to repoint page titles, hints and error lines at a wall ink; those exist because the token layer got night right while page-level text still sat on the wall, and that enumeration is a maintenance tax paid per screen forever.

**Consequence.** A contributor's instinct about a dark theme is wrong here, in both directions: paper tokens move towards black and chrome tokens move towards white inside the same block. Nothing but the checker and this entry defends that, which is why night stops being eyeballed — the values now sit where the checker reads them, which is also what turns the ratios above from opinions into findings. The night block must stay **complete**: any token the light block sets and night re-polarises has to be restated there or the day value leaks through. And the bench shell inherits an obligation the forest partial discharged by hand — the wall belongs to chrome, and nothing but chrome may draw text on it.
