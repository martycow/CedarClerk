# ADR-201 — The window's own controls hang on the drawer lip

**Context.** ADR-188 put the fullscreen toggle in the rail header, on the grounds that the window's
state belongs to the shell and not to a page. The placement, not the ownership, was wrong. ADR-159
reserves the rail's right side for the save state and the screen's one primary action, and a page
publishes both as data. A fullscreen button standing in that row is read as a third thing the page
offers — on the writing sheet it sat directly beside Publish.

**Decision.** `BenchDrawer` gains a `[lipActions]` slot beside the pull, and the shell hangs the
fullscreen toggle there. The lip is the bottom chrome's clickable half — ADR-184 keeps the rule
itself read-only precisely so that the strip above it is where anything gripped goes — and it
belongs to the shell, which is what a window control needs. Controls in the slot are drawn at trim
(ADR-200), since the lip is 32px.

Ownership does not move: `isFullscreen` and its `fullscreenchange` listener stay on the shell, and
the console the drawer happens to hold passes the slot straight through without reading it.

**Consequence.** The rail's right side holds only what a page put there, so the primary action is
unambiguous on every screen. The cost is a slot on a component whose whole design is one pull —
the invitation ADR-184 refused for the rule now exists one strip up, and what may go in it is
"belongs to the window", not "did not fit elsewhere".
