# ADR-157 — A line is not a material: the surface a log line stands on is named by its consumer

**Context.** ADR-138 item 1 enumerates where `data-surface` is spelled, and every bench primitive
ported so far pins one value in its own host block, because every one of them *is* a material:
`app-stamp-badge` and `app-spec-row` are chrome, `app-paper-card`, `app-task-tag` and
`app-leaf-tag` are paper, `app-worktop` is a chrome frame around a paper body, and the two brass
pieces are hardware and spell neither.

`LogLine` is not a material. It paints no ground at all — it is a timestamp, a stamped severity and
one sentence, laid on whatever the container is made of. Two homes are commissioned for it:
`bench-drawer`'s journal, whose region already declares `data-surface="paper"`, and an activity
feed on the hub, which is a sheet inside a shelf panel. A dense strip that wants the same line —
the same journal read at chrome's type — is the third, and it is the one that decides the shape of
the API, because there is no third component to reach for.

**Decision.** `app-log-line` takes `surface: 'paper' | 'chrome'`, defaulting to `paper`, and
reflects it to the host's `data-surface` attribute.

**Attribution.** *This is the implementer's choice under the general instruction to port the
component to both surfaces. It was not put to Marty as a question.* It is reversible by pinning the
attribute, and what it reverts to is one of the two rejected options below, not a second design.

1. **The input moves size and nothing else.** ADR-138 item 6 states the split governs size, never
   contrast, so the row's inks are the same on both settings: the sentence takes `--text`, the
   timestamp and the right-hand anchor `--t2`. ADR-137 rule 5 names `LogLine` timestamps by name
   as a call site the mirror puts on `--t3` and the app does not.

2. **Both settings are declared, so both are measured.** The two blocks are written as
   `:host([data-surface="paper"])` and `:host([data-surface="chrome"])` in the component's own
   stylesheet, which is what puts each under `tools/check-density.mjs` rule 5 — 14px and up on
   paper, 11–13px on chrome. A single block with a `var()` swapped by the input would ship one type
   size the lint can score and one it cannot.

3. **A line is not a control, so it declares no floor.** No `min-height` or `min-width` in either
   block — the same deliberate absence as ADR-155's tally, and for the same reason: chrome's 30px
   is a floor for things you press. Should a control ever land inside a row, it stands at the floor
   its nearest surface declares, and the attribute this input sets *is* that surface — inherited
   through `--hit-surface`, never selected (ADR-156).

4. **The entry test, so this does not become the shape of every component.** A component may take
   its surface as an input only when it paints no ground of its own and is commissioned for homes
   made of different stock. Anything that is a surface — a card, a sheet, a panel, a rail — pins
   its value, and the twelve primitives that shipped before this one stay pinned.

**Rejected.** *Pinning paper and letting a dense strip carry a 14px journal.* Both commissioned
homes are paper today, so this costs nothing today and spends the chrome budget the shell exists to
buy back on the first strip that wants a line. *A second input — `dense`, or a size — beside a
pinned surface.* That is the private density knob ADR-138 rejected in its own words, one level
further down, and it would let a row be dense while the surface it stands on says otherwise.
*Painting a wood-ink variant so a line could lie on the rail.* No call site is commissioned on wood;
inks on wood are `--rail-ink`/`--rail-ink-soft` and the stamp's four tones are mixed against cream,
so that variant is a second ink vocabulary invented for nobody. ADR-147's rule holds here too — the
day a line lies on wood, its pairs get measured then.

**Consequence.** A consumer can now put a line at the wrong density and nothing turns red: both
declarations are legal, and check-density scores what is declared rather than where it is used. The
default is the common case, which is the only mitigation there is. The second cost is that
`data-surface` on this one component describes the container rather than the component, so a reader
grepping for the attribute finds one occurrence that answers a different question from the other
twelve — which is why the entry test in item 4 is written down rather than left to taste.
