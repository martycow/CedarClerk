# ADR-174 — The shell owns the viewport and paints the wall: paper is the surface class, not the ground colour (22.08.2026)

**Context.** The Cedar Bench fidelity audit put the prototype and the app side by side at
`1440x900` in both themes and measured the ground first, because the ground explains most of what
sits on it. The prototype paints the plaster wall under every screen — `#EDE9E0` by day, `#282114`
at night — and the app painted paper: `body` takes `--surface`, and at night that made the page
ground `#D9CEAE`, a light page inside a dark shell. The prototype's rail header spans the full width
with the hook rail under it; the app drew the hook rail full height and the header in a column beside
it. The prototype's shelves run the full height of what is left and its page never scrolls as a
document; the app's did, with the hook rail `position: sticky` to stay in view while it went by.

Three decisions had written the state the audit found, and each had named the condition under which
it would end. ADR-154 put `data-surface="paper"` on the shell's `<main>` so the touch carve-out has a
paper ancestor, and stated its reading as *"paper is the ground, chrome is what stands on it."* The
`body` rule in `styles.scss` painted paper and said the wall *"comes back when a Worktop is there to
hold the paper off it"* — there is one on every ported screen now. ADR-153 clause 6 reserved the
bottom strip's height by padding the root, and its consequence said the scrollbar every short page
gained would end *"when the shell owns the viewport and the pages stop claiming it."* ADR-139
placed the header and the hook rail without saying which one spans the width.

**Decision.** The shell is the viewport, and the wall is what it paints.

1. **`.shell` is `100vh` minus the bottom chrome, paints `--surface-page`, and lays out as a column:
   the rail header across the full width, then a row holding the hook rail and `main.body`.** The
   hook rail is no longer sticky; it is simply as tall as the row. `.body` is a flex column with
   `overflow: auto` — the one scroll container — so a page fills it with `flex: 1; min-height: 0`
   or `height: 100%` and never sizes itself from `100vh`. The three pages that still did
   (`height: calc(100vh - …)`) stop, and the scroll range ADR-153 accepted is gone with them.

2. **`main.body` keeps `data-surface="paper"`, and ADR-154 clause 1 is amended in one respect.**
   The surface class is a density and touch-floor axis (ADR-138, ADR-156), not a paint. Every
   screen still inherits paper's floor from the shell — that is the whole of what ADR-154 bought,
   and `bench-shell.component.spec.ts` still asserts it — but the ground's *colour* is the wall,
   and the worktop, the sheets, the cards and the shelves are what hold paper off it. "Paper is the
   ground" now reads: paper is the surface class the ground declares; the wall is what the ground
   looks like. Clauses 2 and 3 of ADR-154 are untouched.

3. **The open journal is reserved by the shell, not by the root.** ADR-153 clause 6 carries over as
   `--bench-bottom-h` widened on the shell host while `DebugLogService.open` is true, so the row
   shrinks and the drawer never overlays the page. This is the clause that makes the hub reflow with
   the drawer open, and it is kept on purpose: the incident ADR-153 records was an overlay.

**Consequence.** The ground matches the prototype byte for byte in both themes, and night is dark
where it was light. Two things moved with the scroll container. A `position: sticky` element now
sticks to `.body`'s edge rather than the document's — which is how the settings save bar, hidden
under the bottom chrome for the whole port, surfaced floating over the signature buttons the day
the shell took the viewport; it is in flow now, and the settings jump-bar, which does the same thing
one band higher, is on the board. And with the drawer open the hub's Documents shelf can be left a
37px viewport, because reservation shrinks the row — the prototype overlays instead, which clause 3
refuses; which of the two is right is a question for Marty, on the board beside the first.

ADR-139 is amended only in layout: the header spans the width and the hooks hang under it, which
ADR-139 did not pin either way and the prototype does. ADR-154 is amended as clause 2 says and is
otherwise in force. The `body` rule in `styles.scss` keeps painting paper for the four doors outside
the shell, which stand on their own wall (ADR-166).
