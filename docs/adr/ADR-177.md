# ADR-177 — Materials cross to the server too: a second generated list beside the contract (22.08.2026)

**Context.** ADR-137 drew one border around what a server-rendered surface may name: `CONTRACT` in
`cedarclerk-web/tools/contract-tokens.mjs`, thirty-one role names copied into
`CedarClerk.Core/DesignTokens.generated.cs` and inlined by `BlogEndpoints.cs`, `LandingEndpoints.cs`
and `DraftEndpoints.cs`. Its closing sentence is the operative one: *"`CONTRACT` is now the whole
border of what a server-rendered surface may reference; anything outside it must be defined
server-side or not used there."* `check-contrast.mjs` enforces it in both directions — a contract
token that resolves to a gradient fails, and a `var()` in a server sheet naming anything the
generated file does not carry fails.

That border was drawn when the blog was a palette away from the app and a look away from anything.
Porting the blog to Cedar Bench asks it for materials, not roles: the plaster wall it stands on, the
park-sign rail its header is cut from, the paper stock and the paper noise, the brass of a focus
ring, the pine ramp of a button, the leaf a tag is drawn on, the pencil rules of the carpenter's
rule. None of those is a role — `--sheet` says *what a surface is for*, and there is no contract name
that says *what it is made of*. Every one of them exists in `styles.scss` already, measured and
themed, and the app paints with them on every screen.

Two repairs suggest themselves and both are worse than they look.

*Widen `CONTRACT`.* The gradient refusal walks `CONTRACT`, so `--surface-page`, `--surface-rail`,
`--grad-pine`, `--grad-sign-tile` and `--lamp` cannot join it — and those five are most of what makes
wood look like wood. What could join would arrive stripped of exactly the values the look is carried
by, and the blog would re-spell each recipe from its stops. A recipe spelled twice is the hand-kept
duplicate T-101 exists to stop, one level up from the hex it stopped.

*Serve the whole `:root` block.* Cheap — two kilobytes gzipped — and it dissolves the border ADR-137
spent its length arguing for. A blog painting `--wood-groove` would bind a name nothing agreed to
export, and the rename that broke it would be found by a reader, not by a run.

**Decision.**

1. **There are two lists, and they answer two questions.** `CONTRACT` keeps its name, its
   thirty-one entries and every rule ADR-137 gives it: flat colours, no `var()` outside itself, ten
   pinned by `DesignTokenDriftTests`, `--accent` rewritten at runtime. `MATERIALS`, new in the same
   file, names the bench materials a server-rendered surface paints with. Both are copied into
   `DesignTokens.generated.cs` — `Light`/`Dark` for the contract, `MaterialsLight`/`MaterialsDark`
   beside them — and a page inlines whichever it needs.

2. **A material may be a gradient, a texture URL, a shadow or a border shorthand, and that is the
   point of the second list.** The gradient refusal stays on `CONTRACT` and does not walk
   `MATERIALS`. This is safe for the reason the refusal exists: it guards a name a server surface
   paints *where a flat colour is expected* — `--bg` behind text, `--accent` in a stamp — and the
   drift test pins those. A material is painted where its own value is expected; `--surface-rail` is
   a gradient at both ends of the wire.

3. **The off-contract check widens to the union and keeps its teeth.** `check-contrast.mjs` scores a
   `var()` in a server sheet against what `DesignTokens` actually carries, which is now both lists.
   A material renamed in `styles.scss` stops being generated, and the blog's `var()` for it fails
   the run by name — the same protection the contract has, bought by the same mechanism. The stale
   check (*declared in `styles.scss`, missing from `DesignTokens`*) walks the union for the same
   reason.

4. **The entry test for `MATERIALS` is narrower than the contract's, not wider.** A material joins
   because a server-rendered surface paints it *today*, not because it might. The contract's test is
   "the role has to stay nameable"; this one is "the page names it". The list is therefore expected
   to grow by additions that arrive with the call site, and a member with no call site is dead
   weight to be removed, not a reservation. `MATERIALS` carries no pair-table obligation of its own:
   the pair table already measures these tokens for the app, and it measures values, not lists.

5. **Contract names still win at the call site where both name one swatch.** ADR-137 clause 5 is
   unchanged and now has a second consumer. A blog painting a role — body ink, the danger of a failed
   state, the ground, a flat accent fill — takes `--text`, `--danger`, `--bg`, `--accent`. What
   `MATERIALS` may carry, and clause 5 does not forbid, is a *derivation* no contract name holds:
   `--pine-hi`, `--pine-deep` and `--grad-pine` are the lit and shaded faces of one button and are
   mixed from `--pine`, so the ramp travels and follows the user's accent exactly as the app's does.
   `--pine` and `--wall-hi`/`--wall-lo` go with them because those derivations dereference them; what
   stays off the list is every alias that adds nothing — `--paper`, `--paper-2`, `--ink`, `--ink-2`,
   `--ink-3`, which are `--sheet`, `--surface` and the three ink tiers under a second name. The rule
   in one line: a material earns its place by holding a value, never by holding a synonym.

**Rejected.** *One list with a per-name exemption flag.* The flag would be read by two tools and
mean two things — "may be a gradient" and "is not measured as a role" — which is two lists wearing
one name. *Teaching the generator to resolve gradients into flat colours.* It cannot: a gradient is
not a colour, and the surfaces want the gradient. *Leaving the blog to spell the recipes from
contract stops.* Rejected in Context, and it is worth naming again — the recipes are the design
system's, and the copy would be the second one.

**Consequence.** `DesignTokens` grows a second pair of dictionaries and the inlined `<style>` on a
blog page grows by about two kilobytes before compression. The border ADR-137 drew still exists and
still fails a run when crossed; what changed is that it now has two gates rather than one, and the
second one says what a thing is made of. ADR-137 is amended in exactly one respect — its closing
sentence now reads *"`CONTRACT` and `MATERIALS` together are the whole border"* — and is otherwise in
force, clause for clause.
