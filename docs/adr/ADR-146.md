# ADR-146 — A painted colour that reaches the DOM without a stylesheet is a token, not a literal

**Context.** ADR-145 built the pair table around what the app declares, and the census around what the stylesheets say, so between them every foreground-on-background in the product is either covered by a pair or reported as uncovered. Both read stylesheets. A colour bound with `[style.background]` never enters one: Angular writes it straight onto the element, so the walker sees the rule that sets the ink, finds no surface on anything the selector extends, and files it under *the surface is not declared* — one of 489 such halves, indistinguishable from the ordinary case of a rule that inherits its background from a DOM ancestor.

Two components paint that way. The editor's channel list and the admin user list each hashed an identity to an index into a six-colour array declared in the component's own `.ts` (`CHANNEL_COLORS`, `AVATAR_COLORS` — the same six values, copied), and each set an ink in CSS: `#fff` in the editor, `var(--sheet)` in admin. Neither ink cleared 4.5:1 on all six fills. White failed three of them — `#C98A3B` 2.92, `#6EB2F0` 2.26, `#8A6FBF` 4.11 — on an initial at 13px bold; `--sheet` was worse on every fill in both themes. The editor's ink carried a comment asserting white "is the readable foreground in both themes" and that `var(--sheet)` "would go dark and become unreadable", and both halves are false: white fails half the array, and `--sheet` at night is `#E6DCC2`, cream, because paper does not invert (ADR-141). The admin copy of the same palette, three files away, already used the token the comment said was unusable.

No ink repairs this. The fills span the luminance range: a light ink fails on `#C98A3B` and `#6EB2F0`, a dark one fails on `#5B6E46`, `#3E7A4E` and `#B4452C`, and `#8A6FBF` sits close enough to the middle to fail both. The array was the defect, not the ink chosen for it.

**Decision.**

1. **The fills are tokens.** `--avatar-1 … --avatar-6` and `--avatar-ink` join the palette in `styles.scss`, next to `--series-*`, which they resemble: a fixed identity set, assigned by hash and never cycled. The hue of each is kept and the luminance moved until white clears 4.5:1 — `#9C6B2E`, `#5B6E46`, `#3E7A4E`, `#B4452C`, `#4B79A3`, `#8168B3`, measuring 4.61 / 5.58 / 5.12 / 5.49 / 4.60 / 4.60. Three were already clear and are untouched.

2. **One set for both themes.** The fills are declared in `:root` only. An identity that changed colour at night would stop identifying anything, and since the ink is fixed with it, the pair holds in both themes by construction rather than by a second measurement.

3. **One hasher.** `core/avatar-color.util.ts` holds the hash and the initial, and returns `var(--avatar-N)`. Two copies of one palette are what let two different inks ship against the same six colours without anything noticing.

4. **The pair table measures them, and reads the count.** `--avatar-ink` against every `--avatar-N`, floor 4.5 — an initial is text, not the 3:1 a swatch would owe. The fills are discovered by matching `--avatar-\d+` in the palette rather than looping to six, so a seventh fill is measured by the commit that adds it; a palette with none at all throws rather than reporting a vacuous pass.

**Consequence.** The general rule this states is narrower than "no literals": a hex inside a component is acceptable where a stylesheet declares it and the census can therefore see it — the brand fills in `settings.component.css` are other people's colours and stay literals, reported as uncovered and judged on their own terms. What is not acceptable is a painted colour that reaches the DOM through a binding, because no tool in the repository can reach it and no census total will ever list it as missing. Such a colour goes in the palette, where a pair can name it.

That leaves the failure mode this closes: a check whose reported total is honest and whose blind spot is invisible. The census counts 740 rules stating one half of a combination, and an inline-bound fill is not among them — it is not a rule at all. The gate was verified to go red, not merely to exist: restoring `#C98A3B` as `--avatar-1` fails `check-contrast` at 2.92 in both themes and exits 1.

Two surfaces stay outside every check and are named here so they are not rediscovered as findings. `CedarClerk.Server/Email/EmailTexts.cs` builds confirmation mail from inline `style` attributes — `#fff` on `#566842` measures 6.08 and `#686257` on the client's white ground 6.05, both clear, but a mail client that inverts is neither measured nor controllable. And cork, `#C9A46B` in a mirror component, has no token to name (ADR-140).
