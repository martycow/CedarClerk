# ADR-185 — Settings is a board of papers, and its section index stands on the left (22.08.2026)

**Context.** The settings screen is a 700px column on the bare wall: an `app-index-tabs` strip, a
sticky row of anchor buttons under it, and four or five `.settings-card` sheets below. Two defects
Marty reported are the same defect.

The anchor row is `position: sticky; top: 64px` in a **vertical** flow, so as the page scrolls the
chips travel down over the sheets and stand on top of them. A sticky element in a column has nothing
to be sticky against but the content it is indexing — the row and the sheets are competing for the
same 700px of width, and the row wins by `z-index`.

The tabs and the chips are wood and paper hung on nothing. Every other bench surface states what it
stands on: a shelf is a wooden frame with a sheet dropped into it, the worktop is a lit top with one
object on it, the wall is a pegboard. Settings alone puts a carved sign tile straight onto the
plaster, and Marty's word for it is exact — *just flying over nothing*. His own proposal is the
system's own vocabulary: papers on a wooden board, with the tabs cut from the same wood.

Both go away with one change of shape, because a board with a left-hand index has somewhere to put
the index that is not on top of the paper.

**Decision.**

1. **The screen is a board.** `.settings-page` becomes a shelf: `--shelf-frame` wood, a 3px lip, a
   `--wood-edge` hairline and `--shadow-shelf`, with one paper sheet dropped into it. The sheets
   inside keep their own paper faces and their shadows, so what the board holds still reads as
   separate papers laid on it rather than as one printed panel.

2. **The tabs stand on the board's own wood**, at its top edge, where `app-index-tabs` was drawn to
   stand — the component is unchanged and this is the first surface in the app that gives its tiles
   the ground they were cut for.

3. **The section index is a column down the left of the sheet**, not a row across the top of it. It
   is sticky against that column, which is the axis a sticky index belongs on: it can follow the
   scroll for the whole page without ever crossing the content it indexes. Below 900px the board is
   one column again and the index returns to a row — a 180px rail beside a 320px sheet is neither.

4. **The reading column keeps its width.** The board is 940px so that the sheet beside a 180px index
   is still the ~700px that ADR-071's two-bucket rule gives a text-form page. The index is not
   bought out of the reading measure.

**Rejected.** *Making the anchor row non-sticky.* It fixes the overlap by removing the feature: on a
page this long the index is what the row is for. *A second `HookRail` down the settings page.* The
wall means "which screen", and a second one inside a screen would make the same object mean two
things. *Folding the sections into the tabs.* Eight tiles across the top of a 940px board is a strip
you stop reading, which is the same budget argument `HookRail.prompt.md` makes for seven hooks.

**Consequence.** Two of the app's screens now say what they stand on in the same words — the shelf
panels in the shell and this board — and the settings index can never again be drawn over the thing
it indexes, because they no longer share an axis. The `sec-*` ids are untouched, so
`UiInventoryDriftTests` and every anchor into this page still resolve.
