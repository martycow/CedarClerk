# ADR-235 — A team is a second way in, resolved after the first, and a status is not a role (01.09.2026)

**Context.** ADR-217 gave a project collaborators: one `ProjectMember` row per person per project,
resolved by `ProjectAccessResolver`, granting the canvas and nothing else. It works, and it does not
scale to how the maintainer actually works — inviting the same five people to every new project, one
address at a time, with no way to pause somebody without deleting them. T-358.

**Decision.** `Team { OwnerId, Name }` and `TeamMember { OwnerId, TeamId, Email, MemberUserId, Role,
Status, InviteToken, … }`, plus `Project.TeamId`. Five commitments.

1. **A team always belongs to a user, and there is no transfer.** `OwnerId` is an account, never
   another team. That is what keeps every question about a team answerable inside one tenant —
   billing, quotas and the media gate all still key on a single owner, and a team that could own
   itself would break each of them at once.

2. **There is no team admin.** Inviting, roles, statuses and deletion are the owner's alone. The
   moment a member can hand the team on, the team owns people rather than a person owning the team.

3. **Team access is resolved *after* per-project membership, never merged with it.** A person can be
   an editor on the team and a viewer on one of its projects; the narrower grant is the deliberate
   one and must not be widened by the broader one. `ProjectAccessResolver` asks `ProjectMembers`
   first and only falls through to `TeamMembers` when that misses.

4. **A status is a second column, not two more roles.** `active` / `restricted` / `banned`.
   Restricted resolves to a viewer wherever the team reaches; banned resolves to nothing at all and
   clears the pending invite token, so the address cannot walk back in through its own mail. Two
   columns rather than one because restoring somebody has to give back the role they had, and a
   single column would have forgotten it.

5. **Deleting a team takes it away from its projects; it never deletes one.** The projects stay
   whole and private to their owner, which is the only outcome of that button that cannot lose work.

**Consequence.** A `/teams` screen off the tray, a team picker in the project's own edit dialog (one
row: which group reaches this, or nobody — who is *in* a team stays the Teams screen's business,
which is the `ui-changes.md` rule about second homes for one concern), one migration (`AddTeams`),
and the same invitation shape the canvas already had, down to reusing `Canvas.InviteTokenBytes`: one
token shape for every invitation the product sends.

What a team grants is still exactly what ADR-217 froze — the canvas and nothing else. Widening that
is a separate decision about the other screens, not something a team quietly did on the way past.

**Three things this closed on the way.** `T-301`: the shell asks `GET /api/projects/:id/access`
before drawing its wall, so a member sees the doors a membership actually opens instead of seven
that answer 404. `T-302`: "Shared" is a fourth tile on the project hub's own state strip rather than
a screen of its own, because a project somebody shared is still a project and the hub is where a
reader looks — losing the invitation mail no longer loses the project. `T-304`: a live, unspent
invite token stands in for the registration invite code, so somebody invited by name can make the
account the invitation needs; the register screen reads it out of the `returnUrl` and says the field
is unnecessary rather than leaving a stranger hunting for a code.
