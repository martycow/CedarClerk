# ADR-239 — Paper first: the shell after Cedar Bench (01.09.2026)

**Context.** The maintainer read ten screenshots of v0.20.1 on a wide monitor (`docs/screenshots/`)
and ruled the interface unfinished and empty. `docs/design/paper-first/BRIEF.md` names the five
defects: the frame is louder than the content; nothing grows with the screen; twelve equal hooks
with 10px captions are navigation without hierarchy; empty states say nothing; three faces per
screen plus a request/error console at the foot make the app read as a debugger. The eight
artboards beside the brief (`*.dc.html`, rendered as `*.png`) are the drawing this entry ports, and
every value in them is a token value from `cedarclerk-web/src/styles.scss`.

Cedar Bench (ADR-136…176, fidelity pass ADR-174…184) answered a different question — one look
instead of two — and answered it well; what it got wrong is the furniture. The wood rail, the hook
wall, the ruler bar, the drawer lip and the sign-board panel headers are load-bearing in code
(`bench/chrome/*`, `RailActionsService`, `RulerService`, fourteen pages publishing readouts into a
bar) and in tests (`e2e/10-ui-shell`, `16-media`, `17-density` bind `app-rail-header`;
`e2e/helpers.ts` reads "Synced" off `app-ruler-bar`; `tools/check-density.mjs` rule 9 knows six
chrome components by name). Replacing the frame therefore means deciding, clause by clause, which
of the bench's recorded decisions survive as tokens and which die as furniture.

**Decision.** Where an artboard and an earlier ADR disagree, the artboard wins for this port; each
clause below says which entry it supersedes and which it keeps.

1. **The bench chrome is retired.** `bench/chrome/bench-shell`, `rail-header`, `hook-rail`,
   `ruler-bar` and `bench-drawer` are deleted, with their specs, the `brass-nail` peg the hook rail
   drove, and the `resin-drop` save indicator the rail carried. Supersedes ADR-139 clauses 3–5
   (navigation on the hook rail, the placement table, the rail's right slot), ADR-183 (the overflow
   menu on the tool wall), ADR-155 (the tally on the hook), ADR-153 clauses 3–6 (the lip as the
   console's toggle, the status-bar table, the reserved bottom strip), ADR-174 clauses 1 and 3 (the
   header across the width with hooks under it; the journal reserved by the shell), ADR-184 (the
   wood bottom edge), ADR-186's tile (the switcher moves, its memory stays — see clause 5), ADR-188
   and ADR-201 (fullscreen on the drawer lip). ADR-139 clause 1 stands: the shell is still a parent
   route, `app/shell/app-shell.component`, and the pre-auth doors stay outside it by the shape of
   the route tree. ADR-139 clause 6 (no Electron menu bar) stands.

2. **Sign boards go too.** `shelf-panel` loses its carved header and wood frame and becomes a
   plain `.card` with a `.label` caption; `worktop` loses the lamp, the chalk edge and the squared
   grid and becomes the page's content column. Both keep their selectors and inputs so the pages
   that use them restyle rather than rebuild. Supersedes ADR-138 clause 1's census of chrome
   components (the density axis itself survives, clause 3), ADR-160 clause 2 (documents as a
   `ShelfPanel` under the wall — the dashboard's document list is a scrolling card, Main.png) and
   ADR-164 rule 1's index-tile framing where a screen adopts the artboard's `.seg` control instead.
   ADR-164 rule 4 (a right column that is never empty) and ADR-167/168's reading-screen shapes stand
   as layout; their materials change.

3. **What stays.** The tokens, the palette and the contract names (ADR-136, ADR-137, ADR-145,
   ADR-146); the five faces (ADR-143, ADR-180, ADR-191); the accent picker and its derivations
   (ADR-152); light and dark (ADR-141); the surface axis and its numbers (ADR-138 clauses 2–7,
   ADR-156, ADR-182, ADR-200) — the new shell is **paper**, and chrome shrinks to the census in
   `docs/design/paper-first/CONTRACT.md` §B; the contrast and density gates, unchanged in what they
   measure; the blog and the landing (ADR-137's served contract, ADR-179, ADR-215, ADR-231),
   untouched; the document model, the renderers and the publish pipeline; `app-icon` and the
   generated Phosphor set (ADR-142, ADR-214); ADR-163 (a control that navigates is an anchor) and
   ADR-171 (a smoke test binds to role and name). The worktop components `spec-row`, `growth-chart`,
   `module-tile`, `log-line`, `paper-card`, `stamp-badge`, `task-tag`, `leaf-tag`, `index-tabs`,
   `input`, `button` keep their jobs and their APIs and are restyled in place.

4. **The sidebar is the navigation.** A 232px paper column with one hairline right edge: logo and
   wordmark, the project switcher card, three labelled groups — **Write** (Documents, Assets,
   Canvas, Dialogues, Site), **Plan** (Tasks, Planner, Calendar), **Ship** (Builds, Posts,
   Metrics) — then All projects and Settings at the foot, then the user row. 14px labels, 17px
   icons, counts right-aligned, the active item a solid `--accent` plaque with `--text-on-pine` ink.
   Canvas and Dialogues are not on the artboard, which draws a blog project; they are screens the
   app has and belong to Write, which is the rule ADR-160 clause 1 set for tiles. The visibility
   rules of `bench-shell.component.ts` carry over unchanged: with the indie module off the groups
   collapse to Documents (`/drafts`), Assets (`/library`), Calendar, Posts, Metrics; a project
   member sees Canvas and the account-wide screens (ADR-217). **Admin leaves the navigation** and
   lives in the account menu, with Glossary, Presets, Teams, Feedback, Appearance, the theme
   toggle, the debug console, fullscreen, the landing link, `/dev/*` and Logout. Supersedes ADR-151
   (theme and Appearance behind the rail's dots) and ADR-139 clause 4 (the six-hook budget).

5. **The sidebar collapses to a 92px icon rail on the editor and nowhere else.** Editor.png draws
   it: icon over a 12px caption, no counts, the project switcher moved into the editor's top bar.
   The mode is decided by the route, not by width — ADR-147 clauses 2–4 stand: no `@media
   (max-width)` rule enters `app/shell/`, and `T-236`/`T-237` still own the narrow screens. ADR-147
   clause 1 (built to the kit at the kit's proportions) is superseded: the artboards are the
   geometry now, and their one geometry is 1440×900 too. `CurrentProjectService` stays the one
   memory of the open project (ADR-186) and ADR-221 (switching keeps the open screen) stands; only
   the control that reads them moves.

6. **A page has a header, not a sign board.** `app-page-header`: title in `--font-display` 27px, a
   meta line under it (status tag · kind · counts · last edit), the page's one primary action
   right-aligned with at most one secondary beside it. The header is inside the page, so a page
   projects into it directly and nothing is published into chrome any more:
   **`RailActionsService` and `RulerService` are deleted.** The rail's one primary action becomes
   the header's primary slot; the ruler's readouts become the header's meta items on every screen
   but the editor, where they become the document frame's footer. Supersedes ADR-159 clauses 1–3
   and ADR-153 clause 5's signal-holder shape; ADR-159 clauses 4–9 (title on the sheet, the
   inspector as the meta strip's home, the language leaves, the block index) stand.

7. **Content fills the viewport.** `.page` is a flex column of the shell's scroll container; a
   list is a scrolling card, a board column is a full-height column, a calendar cell grows
   (`minmax(0, 1fr)` rows). Nothing declares `100vh` and nothing pins a height in pixels that the
   artboard draws as a fraction. ADR-174 clause 2 stands: `main` keeps `data-surface="paper"` and
   the ground is the wall.

8. **Every empty state names the next action.** `app-empty-state`: a dashed area, one sentence,
   one action; where the page wants a hint it carries a `.margin-note` in `--font-note`. "Nothing
   here", a blank calendar and a dashboard of zeros are defects, not states.

9. **The editor is one document with three tabs.** `app-document-frame` supplies the top bar
   (project switcher, undo/redo, saved state, date, the Publish split button), the title line with
   its status tag, the **Write / Preview / Publish** tabs addressed by `?tab=`, and a footer (Share
   preview, "saved · N words", the next step). Write is today's editor — tool strip, sheet,
   inspector with Document / Languages / Outline. **Preview is new**: destinations with readiness
   on the left (Blog, Telegram, X, Bluesky), the rendered page in the middle (Desktop/Mobile,
   width, RU/EN, theme), the checks for the selected destination on the right; selecting Telegram
   swaps the render to a phone drawn from the real message split and the checks to Telegram's.
   **Publish this sprint opens the existing Export modal** from the tab and from the top-bar
   button; the modal's steps become the tab's body in `T-365`, not now — the modal is 830 lines
   with its own ADRs (ADR-096, ADR-190, ADR-198, ADR-220) and porting it beside a new Preview tab is
   two rebuilds in one lane.

10. **The preview is projected on the server, and nothing is sent.** `GET
    /api/drafts/{id}/preview/telegram?lang=` returns what Telegram would receive — message count,
    per-message blocks as plain text with media references and captions, character counts, cut
    reasons, the CTA buttons of the last message — built from `CedarToTelegramBlocksRenderer`,
    `TelegramThreadSplitter` and `TelegramPublishTarget.ParseCtaButtons`, the same three the send
    path uses, so the phone cannot drift from the wire. `GET /api/drafts/{id}/preview/blog?lang=`
    serves the blog page the reader would see, owner-scoped and per language, which the public
    `/preview/{token}` page does not do (it renders the primary language only and rotates a
    shareable token). Readiness is composed on the client from `GET /api/publish/networks`,
    `GET /api/channels` and the blog host the editor already holds; there is no readiness endpoint,
    because every fact it would carry already has one. Named response records rather than the
    area's anonymous objects, because a preview DTO is consumed by a different lane than the one
    that writes it.

11. **The debug console leaves the working screens.** It is an overlay panel opened from the
    account menu or ``Ctrl+` ``, over the content and never reserving height; the request, in-flight
    and error counts are no longer drawn anywhere on a working screen. `DebugLogService`, the
    interceptor and the entry shape are untouched (ADR-153 clause 1 stands). The overlay incident
    ADR-153 records was a strip covering a status bar; there is no status bar left to cover.

12. **The sprint cut, per route.** Full treatment — shell, header, layout and empty states to the
    artboard: `/projects` (Hub.png), `/projects/:id` (Main.png), `/projects/:id/tasks` (Board.png),
    `/calendar` (Calendar.png), `/editor` (Editor, Preview, PreviewTelegram). Shell, page header,
    fill-the-viewport and empty-state sweep with no artboard: `/drafts`, `/posts` and its Stats and
    Forms tabs, `/library`, `/settings`, `/glossary`, `/presets`, `/teams`, `/admin`, `/invite/:token`,
    `/team-invite/:token`, `/projects/:id/assets`, `/planner`, `/builds`, `/canvas`,
    `/canvas/:boardId`, `/dialogues`, `/dialogues/:scriptId`, `/showcase`, `/dev/styleguide`,
    `/dev/icons`. The doors outside the shell — `/login`, `/register`, `/auth/complete`,
    `/onboarding`, `/terms`, `/privacy`, `/download` — lose their wood and keep their own theme
    toggle (ADR-151 clause 3, ADR-166). What the artboards draw and the data cannot answer is not
    drawn (ADR-160 rule 8's precedent): Main.png's "Where it goes → Telegram" per project has no
    project→channel table, so the row lists the account's connected channels; Hub.png's "This week /
    Needs attention / Next scheduled" strip needs an aggregate the API does not have and is `T-366`.

13. **Deferred, on the board.** `T-365` the Publish tab body (the Export modal's steps as the
    third tab); `T-366` the hub summary strip and its endpoint; `T-367` X and Bluesky renders in the
    Preview tab (the destination rows show their part counts from `GET /api/publish/thread-preview`
    now, the middle pane says the render is coming); `T-368` sidebar counts with no source today
    (Assets, Posts) — a count is drawn only where the shell already holds the number, never as 0.
    `T-236`, `T-237`, `T-265` (narrow screens) are unchanged by this entry.

**Rejected.** *Direction B* (`DirectionB.dc.html`) — keep the wood, fix scaling and empty states;
cheaper, and the frame stays the loudest thing on screen. *The hook rail as a collapsed sidebar
mode* — it is a different object (pegboard, nails, tally), and keeping it would keep the tokens,
the checker census and the `T-236` brief that describe it. *Keeping `RulerService` as the footer's
data source* — the footer is the editor's own and the service existed only because a page could not
reach chrome above the outlet. *A readiness endpoint* — four facts from four tables that each have
an endpoint, joined server-side for one screen. *Preview as a modal* — it is the second of three
states of one document, and a modal is where the Export window already is. *Counts in the header
of every page from a new aggregate call* — the header shows what the page already fetched, the rule
ADR-168 rule 5 set for shelves.

**Consequence.** Five components, two services, their specs and roughly forty `--rail-*`,
`--pegboard`, `--hook-face`, `--sign-tile-*`, `--shelf-frame`, `--frame-board` and `--tex-wood`
declarations go, and each removal is measured, not remembered (ADR-170): `tools/check-contrast.mjs`
loses the pairs that named them and `tools/check-density.mjs` `CHROME_PARTS` shrinks to the census
in the contract. Fourteen pages stop calling `ruler.publish`/`rail.publish` and draw the same
numbers in their own header. Three e2e specs and `helpers.ts` rebind (`app-rail-header`,
`app-ruler-bar`, the "More" button, the "Export" name), each in the lane whose change breaks it.
`docs/design/UI-INVENTORY.md` gains a `## shell` section and loses the bench-chrome rows; ADR-138's
count of chrome components and ADR-139's placement table become history. The narrow-screen
regression ADR-147 records stays exactly as recorded — the sidebar has no collapse below 1440 other
than the editor's route-driven rail — and the brief `T-236` sends to Claude Design has to be
rewritten against this shell before it is run.

What this leaves open: the Publish tab is a door to a modal until `T-365`, so for one sprint the
editor has a tab that does not stay selected; the Preview tab's blog render is an iframe of a
served page, which means the appearance preferences of the editor sheet do not apply to it and
should not; and `T-356`'s verdict on the dialogue tool, if it retires the screen, removes one Write
entry and nothing else in this entry.
