# ADR-179 — The blog is a sheet on the wall under a park-sign rail; the reader gets no tools (22.08.2026)

**Context.** The Cedar Bench port closed every screen of the app (`docs/design/UI-V2-PLAN.md` §0) and
left the one surface a stranger actually sees. The blog already carried the bench *palette* — T-101's
generator has fed it `DesignTokens` since ADR-071 principle 6 — and nothing else: 12px card radii,
pill chips, a sticky white header, `system-ui` under a token that says *Source Sans 3*, and four
emoji standing in for the counts. That is the exact shape of the failure the generator was built to
end, one level up: the colours agree and the look does not.

The kit ships three reference screens — `hub.html`, `writer.html`, `stats.html` — and every one of
them is the tool. There is no blog prototype, and the readme says why one is not needed: the material
rulebook and the density contract decide a surface before a screen draws it. *Wood is the frame,
paper is every reading surface, ink never sits on wood, chrome may be dense and paper may not.* A
public post is the purest case the rulebook has — one sheet, read.

**Decision.**

1. **The wall is the ground and the sheet is held off it.** `body` paints
   `linear-gradient(170deg, var(--wall-hi), var(--wall-lo))` under `--lamp`, which is ADR-174's rule
   restated for a page with no shell to own the viewport: the ground's colour is plaster, and the
   post, the cards, the comment box and the gate are what hold paper above it. `--surface` stops
   being the page background, which is what it had been since the blog had a background at all.

2. **The header is the park-sign rail and the footer is the carpenter's rule.** Both are wood, both
   are chrome, and both take the chrome half of ADR-138: 30px control boxes, 11–13px type, cream
   lettering (`--rail-ink`) over `--surface-rail` with the wood grain over it, Vollkorn for anything
   that reads as carved. The rule at the bottom carries ticks and the mono counts a reader may want
   — views, reactions, comments — because the bottom edge of the bench is where the rule is kept.

3. **The reader gets no tools, and this clause is the refusal.** No hook rail, no shelf panels, no
   bench drawer, no index tabs, no worktop grid. Every one of them is a place to put a command, and
   a reader has no commands: they read one sheet and follow a link. The kit's chrome vocabulary
   enters here only where the blog already had that job — a header and a footer — and the two pieces
   of furniture it does keep are the two the page already stood on.

4. **A post is `writer.html`'s sheet, at the reading numbers.** Paper stock with `--tex-paper` over
   it, `--border-paper`, `--radius-paper` (3px, not 12), the two-layer paper shadow, and no rotation
   — a stack of drafts looks handled, a published page does not lean. Titles are `--font-display`,
   body is `--font-serif` at `--fs-read` / `--lh-read`, and both are now faces the host actually
   serves (ADR-178). The sheet keeps its own width; the index's cards are the same stock, cut
   smaller, with the kit's 2–3px hover lift and nothing else.

5. **A tag is a leaf, a state word is a stamp, and neither is a pill.** `--leaf-bg` / `--leaf-ink`
   with the dried stock for a filter that is off (ADR-176's day/night pair, reused verbatim); the
   showcase's roadmap statuses and the private post's lock become `StampBadge`'s recipe — Vollkorn,
   uppercase, wide tracking, a 1.6px border in its own ink. The `border-radius: 999px` that ran
   through eleven of the blog's classes leaves with them: the rulebook's roundest object is an 8px
   plaque.

6. **No emoji, and the counts are drawn.** 👁 👍 💬 🔒 are replaced by stroke SVG on the kit's 16px
   grid, inline in the markup — the blog cannot reach `assets/cedar-icons.js`, which is bundled with
   the app, and four glyphs do not justify a second delivery mechanism. The numbers beside them go
   `--font-mono`, per the readme's rule that every number does.

7. **Focus is brass, in both layers.** `2px solid var(--brass-edge)` with the `--focus-halo` ring,
   which is ADR-140's pair copied rather than re-derived: it is the one focus style that clears 3:1
   on paper and on rail alike, and the blog has both surfaces on every page.

8. **The theme toggle stays, and stays a paper button at the chrome box.** ADR-175 settled the same
   question for the app's doors — a tinted face on the rail needs wood under it to read as anything,
   and the honest answer at 30px is paper. The blog's toggle is the same control on the same
   reasoning, so the two hosts do not disagree about a button that does one thing.

**Rejected.** *A hook rail down the left of the blog.* It is the navigation of a tool with five
screens; the blog has two, and one of them is a list of the other. *Deckled edges on the post sheet.*
The tear says draft; a published post is the one thing on the bench that is finished. *Rotating the
index cards.* Handled-stack whimsy on a page a stranger lands on first reads as a rendering bug, and
the readme puts whimsy in margin notes rather than in the furniture. *A worktop under the post.* The
grid is squared paper for a thing being worked on, and it would put a second paper texture behind the
first. *Keeping the pills until a later pass.* Two radius vocabularies on one page is how the blog
fell behind the first time.

**Consequence.** The blog names about fifty tokens it could not name before, which is what ADR-177
exists to allow, and the contrast census now measures a page with two ink polarities on it — cream on
rail, ink on paper — where it previously measured one. Night needs nothing special: paper stays cream
and the wall darkens, per ADR-141, and the blog inherits that by inheriting the tokens.

Three things this port does not touch, so nobody looks for them. The post *body* markup is
`CedarToBlogHtmlRenderer`'s and its element vocabulary is unchanged — this decision restyles what it
emits and adds no node type. The watermark and copy-guard overlays keep their geometry, including the
radius they clip to, which now matches the sheet's 3px rather than the old 12. And the reading
column's width is left where it was: 760px is a measured line length, not a leftover, and the kit's
640px sheet is a viewport inside a tool rather than a page in a browser.
