# ADR-172 — The current-tool bar is measured, and it is the reinforcement rather than the cue (20.08.2026)

**Context.** Stage 6, the closing sweep of the Cedar Bench port. The census run surfaced one combination the pair table
never covered: the brass bar that marks the current tool on the hook rail
(`hook-rail.component.ts`, `.hook.is-current .here`). It is a shape and not a label, so it owes the
3:1 of WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 rather than a text floor, and the surface it owes it against is the wall
and not the tile — it is offset past the hook's right edge and stands on the pegboard.

Measured, it reads **4.42:1 at night and 2.91:1 by day**, the day figure taken at the lightest stop
of the pegboard against the lightest stop of the brass. It misses by 0.09, in one theme, at one end
of two ramps.

Three facts decided what to do with that, and each was measured rather than assumed:

- **The raised sign tile is relief, not colour.** `--grad-sign-tile` scores **1.00:1** against the
  pegboard and 1.00–1.22:1 against a resting hook's `--hook-face`: at the stop that bounds it, the
  current tile is the same tone as the wall it hangs on. What makes it read as raised is
  `--shadow-shelf` and the ramp across its own face. So the tile cannot be named as the colour cue
  the bar falls back on — it is not one.
- **The caption is the cue, and it already passes.** `.hook.is-current .cap` is `font-weight: 700`
  against a resting hook's normal weight, and the pair `--rail-ink` on `--grad-sign-tile` — the
  caption carved into the tile — clears its floor in both themes. A weight change on a passing
  ink is a visual difference that owes no ratio of its own.
- **The bar is not the state as far as anything but an eye is concerned.** It carries
  `aria-hidden="true"`, and `aria-current="page"` is on the hook itself, so the state reaches
  assistive technology by the attribute that exists for it.

**Decision.**

1. **The pair is added and kept**, as `--grad-brass` on `--pegboard`, floor 3.0, note *current-tool
   bar against the wall*. The gap this closes is that the bar shipped through the whole port with
   nothing measuring it; a bar that later darkens, or a pegboard that later lightens, now has
   something to go red.
2. **The day shortfall is an accepted exception, in the form ADR-140 already uses** — an `except`
   string on the pair naming this ADR, so it prints as `xfail` with its reason attached and the
   number stays visible in every run rather than being rounded away.
3. **The floor is not lowered and the pair is not narrowed.** 3.0 is what a graphical object owes;
   writing 2.9 to make one value fit would leave every future bar unmeasured, which is the failure
   the pair exists to catch.
4. **Neither `--grad-brass` nor `--pegboard` is re-derived to close 0.09.** Both are load-bearing
   across the chrome — the brass ramp is the ruler, the drawer pull and one of ADR-140's own three
   focus-ring exceptions; the pegboard is the ground of the whole tool wall. Moving either to gain
   a hairline on a reinforcing bar changes surfaces that are measured and passing today, and a
   palette change at the close of a port is a design decision rather than a cleanup.

5. **An exception names the theme it applies to.** Mutating `--grad-brass` down to the wall's own
   tone showed the night pair falling to 1.52 and still printing `xfail`: `except` was a bare
   string, consulted in both themes, so accepting a day shortfall also bought silence at night for
   a value that passes there by a wide margin. That is an exception narrowing what the check looks
   at, which is the one thing this tool may not do. `except` now carries `exceptIn`, and a pair is
   only forgiven in the theme it names — the three ADR-140 exceptions are scoped to `light` as
   well, which changes nothing they report today and makes a night regression in any of the four
   go red instead of printing as accepted.

**Consequence.** The exception count goes from three to four, and all four are graphical-object
floors on the brass — which is the honest shape of this palette: brass is the metal the chrome uses
for indication, and it is a mid-tone against mid-tone wood by day. If the day chrome is ever
re-derived, these four are one question and should be answered together rather than one at a time.
The cost of accepting is that a person who cannot resolve the bar reads the current tool from the
bold caption and the shelf shadow; the cost of not accepting would have been a palette change
nobody asked for.
