# ADR-228 — A queue slot's occurrence is its ISO week, and the send counter rides the slot id (29.08.2026)

**Context.** Wave 2 item 10: `QueueSlot` names a weekly moment on one destination, and `FillQueueSlotsJob` (every 30 minutes) keeps upcoming occurrences filled from the owner's evergreen pool as ordinary `ScheduledPost` rows — firing is unchanged, `PublishDueScheduledPostsJob` remains the only sender. The rub is the calendar: a slot-filled ticket can be dragged to another day, which keeps its `SlotId` but moves `ScheduledAtUtc`. If "this occurrence is taken" meant "a row exists at the slot's exact timestamp", every drag would vacate the timestamp and the next job pass would fill the same week a second time — the author's rescheduling read as an opening.

**Decision.**

1. **Occupancy is keyed by (SlotId, ISO year+week)**, computed via `ISOWeek` — a weekly slot has one occurrence per ISO week, and that pair is its identity, wherever inside the week the row was dragged. `Failed` rows do not occupy: a failed slot send frees its week for another pick.
2. **The fill is bounded and polite**: occurrences are materialised at most `Horizon` (7 days) out and never closer than `MinLead` (2 hours) — a slot two hours away is close enough that a hand-scheduled post should win it, not the pool. A draft already Pending toward the same target is never queued to it twice; an empty pool leaves the occurrence unfilled, which the calendar draws as an open resin ticket rather than hiding.
3. **`EvergreenSendCount` increments when a slot-filled row actually goes Sent** — by the publish job, keyed on the row's `SlotId` — never at fill time. The pool picks least-sent-first (ties by id), so the counter must mean "reached readers": counting at fill would let a deleted or failed fill burn a send and skew every subsequent pick.

**Consequence.** Dragging a ticket means what the author meant — the week stays filled by that ticket, and the pool does not stuff a second post beside it. The accepted edge: dragging a ticket into a *different* ISO week both frees its origin week (the job may refill it) and occupies the destination week — behavior that follows directly from the key and is intended. The DST caveat is the flip side of storing a UTC weekday+minute pair: the slot's local wall-clock time drifts by an hour across a transition, and the queue panel's form says so instead of pretending otherwise.
