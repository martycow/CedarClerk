# ADR-313 — The Telegram preview shows what the send path sends: one message unless the author chose a thread

Amends ADR-086 (preview side only) and ADR-239 clause 10.

## Context

Three reports against the editor's Telegram preview (issues #3, #5, #7) share one cause: the phone drawn in the Preview tab was built to be a thread preview with a plain-text projection, while the send path is a single `sendRichMessage` with native blocks.

- **#3.** The preview always ran `TelegramThreadSplitter.Split`, cutting a post at the 3,000-character part budget (ADR-086) and drawing a gap between the bubbles. The real send splits only when the author ticks "Publish as a thread" (`SplitIntoThread`, default false, offered only when a blocking `too-long` or `too-many-media` issue exists). A post under 32,768 characters is one message on the wire and two on the screen.
- **#5.** The theme toggle reached only the blog frame. The phone took its colours from the app's paper tokens, which by design stay cream at night (`styles.scss`, "Night keeps the paper cream").
- **#7.** Every photo was clipped to 220px with `object-fit: cover`, and lists were flattened to unmarked lines (`List_items_are_joined_with_newlines`), although the send path emits native `InputRichBlockList`.

## Decision

1. **One message by default.** `GET /api/drafts/{id}/preview/telegram` takes `thread` (default `false`). Without it the whole document is one message (`TelegramThreadSplitter.Whole`); with it the preview splits exactly as the send does. The Preview tab passes the editor's existing `splitIntoThread` signal, so the two cannot disagree.
2. **The message limit is the counter.** The bubble prints `characters / MaxCharacters` (32,768) when not threading, and the part budget only when threading.
3. **The "Show more" fold is drawn, not split.** ADR-086 measured the client collapsing a channel post somewhere between 1,787 and 6,412 characters. The projection carries `FoldAfterCharacters` (`Consts.Telegram.ThreadPartChars`, 3,000), and the phone draws a dashed "Show more" marker inside the single bubble once the text passes it. This keeps the information ADR-086 was after without implying a second post.
4. **The phone has its own palette.** Telegram's light and dark colours (background, bubble, text, link, meta) are CSS variables scoped to the phone and switched by a `theme` input driven by the toolbar toggle. The app's paper tokens are no longer used inside the phone. The palette is listed in `docs/design/DESIGN.md` as a preview-only family.
5. **Images keep their shape.** Photos render at their natural aspect ratio with a maximum height and `object-fit: contain`. A slideshow or collage shows every image it will send; more than four collapse into a "+N" tile.
6. **Lists are projected with structure.** `TelegramPreviewBlock` gains `Items` (text, ordinal, checkbox state), and the phone draws real `<ul>` / `<ol>` / checklists. This is the one place clause 10's "plain text" is extended; inline marks (bold, links, spoiler) are still flattened and stay a known gap (backlog).
7. **A kitchen-sink post is a test.** `TelegramPreviewProjectionTests` projects one document with every block type and the three kinds of list, and asserts that nothing the renderer produced is lost on the way to the phone; the phone's spec carries the matching DTO. A block the preview drops fails a test instead of a reader.

## Consequences

The phone now shows what the wire carries. A post over 32,768 characters shows one over-long message plus the existing blocking `too-long` check, until the author turns on the thread option. `TelegramPreview` gains `FoldAfterCharacters`; list blocks gain `Items`. Tests that expected two bubbles for 4,000 characters now expect one, and a threaded variant keeps the two-bubble case covered. The authoritative check for rendering remains a real send to the test channel (`.claude/rules/telegram-bot.md` §5), replaced for authors by ADR-315.
