# ADR-200 — Trim: the third box, for chips and for controls riding a chrome band

**Context.** ADR-138 split the bench into two densities, chrome at 30px and paper at 38px, and
ADR-182 separated the box a paper control is drawn at from the floor a finger is owed. Two kinds of
control fit neither number, and both shipped visibly wrong.

A chip is one of them. The mirror draws `LeafTag` at 24px — 12px type, 3px of vertical padding —
and the port gave it `--hit-target`, so a tag filter arrived half again as tall as the thing it was
copied from. A row of them reads as a row of buttons, which is what a filter strip must not be.

A control that rides inside a chrome band is the other. `--bench-panel-hd` is 30px and
`--hit-chrome` is 30px, so a search field or an action button dropped into a shelf header is
exactly as tall as the header it stands in and splits the wood open. The mirror's own kits put a
22px tab and a 22px compact field inside that same 30px band; nothing in it is ever drawn at the
band's full height.

**Decision.** A third box, `--hit-trim: 24px`, with two jurisdictions and no others:

1. **Chips.** `LeafTag` and anything that reads as one — a filter leaf, a dried planned-network
   plate, a source key beside a chart series.
2. **Passengers on a chrome band.** Anything projected into `ShelfPanel[actions]`, the index tiles,
   and the compact field. A band draws its own furniture below its own height.

Trim carries `--text-chrome-sm` (11px) or `--fs-12`, never paper's 14px, because it is chrome-side
type by placement even when the stock underneath is paper.

Trim is a **drawing size only**. Under `@media (pointer: coarse)` a trim control spends
`--hit-surface` exactly as every other control does, so a finger is still owed 44px on paper and
30px on chrome. This is the ADR-182 split applied a second time, not an exemption from it.

`tools/check-density.mjs` gains the tier: `--hit-trim` is a contract token pinned at 24px, and the
paper and chrome floor rules accept 24px from a block that names the trim class, the same way
ADR-196's two exceptions are named rather than inferred.

**Consequence.** Filter strips, panel headers and tab rows read at the mirror's proportions instead
of at button scale, and the vertical budget a bench screen spends on chrome stops growing every time
a control is added to a header. The cost is a third number in the contract: a control now has to be
placed before it can be sized, and "which box is this" has three answers rather than two. The guard
is what keeps that from being a matter of memory.
