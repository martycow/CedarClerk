# ADR-154 — The shell's content region is paper, so the touch carve-out has something to stand on

**Context.** ADR-138 item 5 says the coarse-pointer rule "gains a paper qualifier so
`[data-surface="chrome"]` and its descendants are exempt", and `tools/check-density.mjs` rule 6
enforces that every selector under `@media (pointer: coarse)` names `data-surface`. Item 1 of the
same ADR enumerates where the attribute is spelled: the six chrome parts, and, on the paper side,
"worktop sheet, cards, forms, task tags, the editor sheet".

That enumeration is a Stage 4 list. `T-219` lands in Stage 3, and at that point no product screen is
inside a worktop and none of the fifteen page roots spells the attribute at all. Qualifying the rule
against that tree would have satisfied the lint and reached nothing: the six unqualified selectors
it fails on today (`styles.scss`, plus one block each in `admin`, `drafts`, `posts-manager` and
`settings`) all govern controls on page bodies, and a paper qualifier with no paper ancestor above
them is the same thing as deleting the rule. The touch floor `T-082` measured on Marty's iPad would
have gone quiet, with a green check over it — which is exactly the failure `.claude/rules` forbids
buying.

**Decision.** `bench-shell.component`'s content region — the `<main>` the router outlet renders
into — carries `data-surface="paper"`.

**Attribution.** *This is the implementer's choice under the general instruction to proceed with
Stage 3. It was not put to Marty as a question.* It is reversible in one attribute, and the thing it
would revert to is the broken state described above, not a second design.

1. **Paper is the ground, chrome is what stands on it.** Every screen renders on paper unless a
   component nested inside says otherwise, rather than paper being a list of components that
   remembered to declare it. That is the reading of ADR-138 that makes the split enforceable before
   the screens are ported, and it does not contradict item 1 — the sheets and cards named there
   still declare their own attribute, and a projected paper control's nearest `[data-surface]`
   ancestor is still itself.

2. **The coarse-pointer block gains a second clause, and the order is load-bearing.** Chrome nests
   inside paper — a shelf panel stands on the worktop, the dots panel hangs off the rail — so both
   selector lists match a chrome control. They carry equal specificity, which leaves source order to
   decide, and the chrome clause is written second so it wins. Without it the touch floor would push
   the ruler from 30px to 44 and the rail past `--bench-rail-h`, which is the whole of what ADR-138
   was written to prevent.

3. **The four pre-auth pages are outside the shell and therefore outside the rule.** `/login`,
   `/register`, `/terms` and `/privacy` have no paper ancestor, so their controls lose the 44px
   floor. Three of the four are one column of prose with at most two buttons; the cost is real and
   small, and the fix — `data-surface="paper"` on those four page roots — belongs to whoever ports
   them, not to a rule that would have to reach outside the shell to find them.

**What was rejected.** *Leaving the rule global and exempting chrome with `:not()`.* A negation that
has to name every chrome ancestor is a list that goes stale silently, and check-density cannot read
it as a paper qualifier. *Spelling the attribute on all fifteen page roots now.* Fifteen edits that
Stage 4 deletes again as each screen moves into a worktop, and a page root is the wrong owner: the
ground is the shell's, which is why one attribute replaces fifteen. *Narrowing check-density rule 6
to skip the page files until Stage 4.* That is buying a green check with a smaller check, and it is
the one thing the house rules refuse outright.

**Consequence.** One attribute now decides whether a whole app's worth of touch targets measures.
`bench-shell.component.spec.ts` asserts it directly, and states in the test why, because the failure
mode is silent: remove the attribute and every density rule still passes while every button on every
screen quietly drops to its declared size on a tablet. The second cost is that the rule now depends
on the shell existing — a screen rendered outside it, today or by some future route added beside
`/login`, is unmeasured. That is the same boundary the shell already draws for the drawer and the
rule, and it is drawn by the route tree rather than by a URL list.
