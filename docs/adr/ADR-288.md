# ADR-288 — A custom accent is gated at 3:1 on the paper; an area preset is a macro over the sheet controls (05.09.2026)

**Context.** T-047 (B16). The Appearance panel offers five accent presets, each with a day and a
night tone vetted by `tools/check-contrast.mjs` (ADR-141), and a stored hex outside that list snaps
to the bench accent. An author who wants their own colour has nowhere to put it. The same panel
has three sheet controls — measure, text size, line height — and no way to set them to "roughly
what Telegram shows" without knowing the numbers.

**Decision.**

1. **The swatch row gains one more disc: a native colour input.** It writes to the same
   `accentLight`/`accentDark` field the presets use, for the theme currently painted, and travels
   through the same debounced account save. There is no new field: a stored accent is either a
   preset's day hex (the key to both tones) or a custom hex.
2. **A custom accent is refused below 3:1 against the paper.** `accentContrast` scores the hex
   against `--surface` — the deepest cream of the theme (ADR-141), mirrored in `PAPER_SURFACE`
   because the check runs for a theme that may not be the one painted. Under
   `ACCENT_MIN_CONTRAST` the panel does not preview it and says the measured ratio inline
   (`role="alert"`, `aria-invalid`, `aria-describedby`). The same floor applies on load, so a
   stored colour that fails falls back to the bench accent instead of painting. 3:1 is WCAG
   1.4.11's floor for a control boundary; the shipped presets stay at 4.5:1 in the checker — a
   preset is a promise, a custom colour is the author's own choice, and the gate only stops what
   is illegible.
3. **The ink over the accent follows its luminance.** `accentInk` picks `--text` above the 0.179
   crossover and `--sheet` below it; the injected rule sets `--accent-ink` beside `--accent` in
   both themes, and `styles.scss` declares `--accent-ink: var(--sheet)` as the default. Nothing
   painted by a preset changes. Components that paint sheet ink on an accent fill still name
   `--sheet`; moving them to `--accent-ink` is a separate sweep, and until then a bright custom
   accent is legible on the paper but its buttons carry the paper ink — which is why the floor
   above is 3:1 and not lower.
4. **An area preset is a macro, not a setting.** `core/area-presets.ts` holds one constant of
   four — Telegram (narrow, 15/1.45), iPhone (narrow, 17/1.5), iPad (normal, 17/1.6), Blog (wide,
   17/1.75, the blog's `--fs-read`/`--lh-read` on its post column). Picking one in the
   `app-select` writes those three existing preferences through the existing preview; nothing
   about the preset is stored, and the select reads back whichever preset the three controls
   currently add up to, or Custom. Density is a page mode (ADR-071 principle 3) and the typeface
   is the author's (ADR-073), so neither is in the macro; Departure Mono keeps its pixel size.

**Rejected.** *A separate `accentCustom` field* — a second place for the same fact, and every
reader of the accent would have to know which one wins. *Reading `--surface` off the live
document* — only the painted theme is readable, and the gate has to answer for both. *A stored
`areaPreset`* — it would drift from the three values the moment one slider moves, and then be a
setting that lies. *Presets that also set the typeface* — ADR-073 already ruled that the sheet's
typeface is not the system's to change.

**Consequence.** `appearance.service.ts` exports the colour maths (`relativeLuminance`,
`contrastRatio`, `accentContrast`, `accentInk`, `storedAccent`, `resolveAccent`) with specs;
`area-presets.ts` is the one list of presets, with specs for the macro and the match. New
strings live in the appearance i18n block in both dictionaries. `docs/design/UI-INVENTORY.md`
carries the two new rows.
